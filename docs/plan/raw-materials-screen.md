# The Raw Materials screen

Part of [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md), the project guide: the Raw Materials screen as it is now. When each part came in, who asked for it and what it replaced are in the [build plan](build-plan.md)'s dated log. "The app's notes" means [maui-app.md](maui-app.md); a section named without a file (Domain Model, Application Architecture, Reactive Costing Cascade) is PLAN.md's. How the one Save writes a whole material, and how a refresh keeps unsaved units, is in Application Architecture ("One Save writes the whole material").

**The screen is the Blazor page `Components/Pages/RawMaterials`** (since 2026-11-07), over its state. Its list is a `FolderTree`, its tables typed into `LineGrid`s, its pickers `PickField`s and its boxes `NumberField`s and `TextField`s ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md), "How it's built").

## The screen

- **The screen reads and writes through its use cases** (docs/adr/0002, phase 2, 2026-11-20): `IRawMaterialQueries` (the view's catalog and the products using each open material, read side by side; a material's details; the copy a Save reads back; the price history; a sub-assembly's lines; the Dependents shares), `IRawMaterialEditor` (Save, with the material's, a unit's and a brand price's checks, the cost cascade running in it; Delete, Rename, Move, a price history entry's Delete) and `IRawMaterialCategoryEditor` (the list's folders and the Category picker's new category), in `TinapayanRMS.UseCases/RawMaterials/`. No class of the screen holds a repository.
- **One screen for ingredients and packaging** (`RawMaterials`/`RawMaterialsState`): the list beside a strip of tabs, a material open in each with its cards (since 2026-11-20). A material's type and source decide which cards and fields apply.
- **Each material opens in a tab of its own** (the user's, 2026-11-20, as Sub-Assemblies'): the view (`RawMaterialsState`, a `RecordViewState`) keeps the list, the materials, categories and units of measure, Settings' cost buffer and the load; each tab holds a `RawMaterialForm`, the form and its parts, with its own edits and undo, drawn by `RawMaterialFormView`. A click in the list opens the preview tab, a double-click or an edit keeps it, and New and the list's Copy start one in the preview tab. The form's pickers that leave out what it holds (`LinkTargets`, `AddInOptions`, `CategoryOptions`) are its own lists over the view's caches, let go of as its tab closes. The load reads the products using each tab's material, and merges into every form. Delete closes the material's tab. The developer manual's "A record view opens each record in a tab of its own" has the pattern.
- **The layout, which Products and Sub-Assemblies share**: 4px between cards (`record-screens.css`). The list card is on the left, 404px wide (`.tree-card`), and the details take the rest.
  - **The header card** (`SummaryHeader`) stays put.
  - **The details card** sits 4px under it: a card (`.tabs-card`) around an `SfTab` with its tabs along the bottom (`HeaderPosition.Bottom`), the card its only frame. Each tab's content is padded 12px (`.tab-card`) rather than framed again, and scrolls on its own (`.details-tabs .e-content`), so the strip stays at the bottom. Between picking a material and its details arriving, the tabs ignore the mouse and fade (`AreDetailsReady`, `.details-pending`).
- **Each card is a component of its own in `Pages/RawMaterials/`**: `RawMaterials.razor` draws the list card, over the view's list part (`RawMaterialsState.List`), and the tab strip; `RawMaterialFormView.razor` names the form's (`SummaryHeader`, then the details' tabs), each over the tab's form.
  - New puts the cursor in Name (`RedrawNowAsync`, then the field's focus).
- **The screen state is one class over seven parts** (PLAN.md's Conventions, "A screen's part owns its tab"), each a property of it: `List` (`RawMaterialsList`: the tree, the search, the menus, the moves and the naming in place), `PurchaseCost` (`RawMaterialPurchaseCost`: the brand prices, Basis, the buffer and its target, Per, and the header's saved costs), `Units` (`RawMaterialUnits`: the alternative units), `Recipe` (`RawMaterialRecipe`: a made ingredient's sub-assembly, its lines, the add-ins and the batch's totals), `UsageExample`, `PriceLog` (Price History) and `Dependents`. The screen keeps the form's own fields, which material it shows, loading, Save and the undo history over all of them. Each part asks the screen what it needs through an interface the screen implements explicitly (`IRawMaterialsListHost`, `IRawMaterialPurchaseCostHost`, `IRawMaterialUnitsHost`, `IRawMaterialRecipeHost`, `IRawMaterialPriceLogHost`).
  - **The subscriptions that reach from one part to another, or to the form, are the screen's**, made in its constructor in the order they always ran (a brand row's price reaching the texts, the units repricing the brands, the weight loss reaching the batch's totals, a pick loading the details); a part subscribes only to what it holds alone. Order matters there: two subscriptions to one property run in the order they were made, and `LoadLatest` starts its first load as it's subscribed.

## The list

- **A tree of folders** (`RawMaterialsListCard`, a `FolderTree` over `RawMaterialsList.RawMaterialTree`, `RawMaterials/RawMaterialTree.cs`), built and behaving as the Products list does (the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md)) on `FolderTree`, which hold for all three lists).
  - **Two type folders, Ingredients then Packaging**, each holding a folder for every category of that type, by name, and each of those its materials, by name. A Made category's folder sits in Ingredients (`RawMaterialFolderNode.CategoryType`). **There's no Uncategorized**: every material has a category.
  - Each folder shows its material count. A search box over the tree (a DynamicData `Filter` on the cache, `VisibleRawMaterials`) shows only the folders holding a match, opened; the unfiltered `RawMaterials` feeds the pickers.
- **Every folder starts open, and the ones left closed are remembered** (`RawMaterials.ClosedFolders`, keys "Ingredient", "Packaging" and "Ingredient/12", through `FolderMemory`), so a category added since opens too. While no closed folders are kept, it reads the open folders the list kept before (`RawMaterials.OpenFolders`) once. Expand All and Collapse All on a type's folder act within that type; Collapse All leaves the type folders open.
- **Dragging materials onto a category folder, or onto a material in one, moves them there at once** (`MoveMaterialsCommand`, `IRawMaterialEditor.MoveAsync`).
  - `MoveAsync` sets the category alone, for any number of materials in one save, stamps `UpdatedAt`, and refuses a category that no longer exists or isn't of the material's kind (`CategoryTypeFor`, Domain Model). A category costs nothing, so nothing cascades.
  - **A material's type is fixed**, so a drop into the other type is refused, and so is a drop on a type's folder, which has no category to give. Made ingredients and the others never move together.
  - The notice under the tree says what moved, with Undo (`MoveNoticeBar`): "Moved Bread Flour to Ingredients › Dry Goods", "Moved 3 materials to Ingredients › Dry Goods" or "Moved 3 materials".
  - On the material the form shows, the Category picker follows and every undo step takes the new category (`History.RewriteAll`). Anything else typed stays unsaved.
- **Right-click menus** (`RawMaterialsList.TreeMenuFor`, drawn by `TreeMenu`, a Syncfusion `SfContextMenu`):
  - **A material**: Move to ▸ its kind's categories (its own greyed), Rename (F2), Copy and Delete (Del). Rename saves the name alone at once (`RenameAsync`). **Several materials**: only Move N Materials to, and nothing when they're of different kinds, since there's no category they could share.
  - **A type's folder**: New Ingredient Here or New Packaging Material Here, New Folder, New Made Folder (Ingredients only), Expand All and Collapse All.
  - **A category's folder**: New … Here (New Made Ingredient Here in a Made category), New Folder (a category of its own kind), Rename Category (F2) and Delete Category (Del).
  - **The empty space**: New Raw Material, New Ingredient Folder, New Made Folder, New Packaging Folder, Expand All and Collapse All. A toolbar under the search box holds New Raw Material, New Folder (a `MenuButton` offering the ingredient, Made and packaging kinds), Expand All and Collapse All.
  - **New … Here** starts a new material of the folder's type, in its category for a category's folder: `ClearForm(type, categoryId, source)` sets them before the form settles, so it doesn't open with something to save.
  - **Names are typed in place, as on Products** (the Products notes, "Names typed in place"; `RawMaterialsList.TreeNameEdit`, `TreeNameBox`). Rename and Rename Category (F2, or a slow second click on the material picked) turn the row's name into a box, saved at once by Enter or leaving it, given up by Esc. New Folder adds a "New folder" row last in its type's folder (`RawMaterialTree.PendingFolder`, `IsNewFolder`), its tooltip the kind it's for, and the name typed adds the category (or picks the one of that name of its kind). The prompts (`PromptForName`) are gone from this screen.
  - **New and New … Here add the new material's row** (`RawMaterialsList.NewInList`, `RawMaterialTree.PendingMaterial`, `RawMaterialLeafNode.IsNew`): "New ingredient", "New made ingredient" or "New packaging material", last in its category's folder, or, from the header or the empty space, with no category yet, last in its type's folder. **The name typed goes into the form, not the database**, as an undo step that Save is offered for (`NameNewMaterial`): a material needs more than a name (a category, a packaging material's base unit, a made ingredient's sub-assembly). The row keeps the name until Save files the material, when its own row takes over, or until the form leaves it (`ClearForm` ends it: New again, Copy, Don't Save, Delete). It has no menu, isn't dragged, isn't counted and loads nothing when picked. Copy still fills the form and puts the cursor in Name.
  - **Copy** (`CopyMaterialCommand`, `StartCopyOf`) is New … Here for the material's own category, then its name with " Copy" (" Copy 2" and on past a name in use, told apart without case, `CopyNameFor`) and, for packaging, its base unit, set after the form settles, so the copy opens with something to save and leaving it asks. Nothing else comes with it: no prices, units, buffer or add-ins. A made ingredient's copy is made (its Made category holds nothing else) with no sub-assembly; a linked one's is purchased. Nothing is saved until Save, and it asks about the unsaved changes of the material showing first, unlike Sub-Assemblies' Copy, which holds the edits.
  - **Delete Category is offered only for a category no material is in.** Otherwise it reads "Delete Category (holds 4)" and is greyed, and Del does nothing. It counts every material, not a search's matches: a material must have a category, and the foreign key refuses the delete anyway.
- **What the three lists share**: `RecordList` (the three lists' base, each list a part of its screen: the rows picked and what a pick does, the naming in place, the move notice), `ListTreeNode` (each tree's nodes' base: title, icon, folder or not, and a folder's `Count`), `ListCardBase` (the cards' base, with what a row shows), `TreeMenuItem`, `TreeMenu`, `TreeSelection`, `TreeNaming` (the naming in place: the row named, Enter and Esc's rules, a new row taken out), `TreeLeaves`, `Trees/TreeRows` (`LayFolders`, `LayTopFolder`, `RemoveLeaving`), `FolderMemory`, `MoveNoticeBar`, and the drag and drop, menus, F2, Del and Enter in `FolderTree`, through `IListTreeScreen`.
- Tests: `RawMaterialTreeTests`, `RawMaterialsStateTreeTests` (every menu, and each item run as the menu runs it) and `RawMaterialMovePersistenceTests`.

## The header

- **The title row**: the material's name (`HeaderTitle`), then New, Save, Undo, Redo and Delete; a divider under it.
- **The figure tiles**, each in its kind of figure's colour (`.figure-tile` and a `tile-…` colour), a plain row as compact as the Products header's:
  - **Base Unit**: the saved one, "gram (g)" for an ingredient (`SavedBaseUnitText`).
  - **Current Cost per** and **Buffered Cost per** the Per unit picked on Purchase Cost Setup (`HeaderCostUnitText`): the saved costs times that unit's multiplier, to 4 decimals on the base unit, whose costs are fractions of a centavo, and to the centavo on any other (plain getters, worked out as they're drawn). A made or linked ingredient has no Per unit, so its header is per gram. A cost is shown as the bare value everywhere on the screen, never "₱0.0372 / g": the labels already say what it's per.
  - **Total Weight** and **Total Cost**, for a made ingredient only (below).
  - **Last Updated**: when the material's cost last moved, to the day (`RawMaterial.CostUpdatedAt`, `LastUpdatedText`). `TinapayanRMSDbContext.SaveChangesAsync` stamps it from the change tracker, comparing each entry's original costs, since a cost is written from too many places to stamp one by one (a brand price, a buffer edit, a recipe or weight-loss change, a unit's multiplier, the cascade), and a save that rewrites the same figures leaves it alone. `UpdatedAt`, when the form last saved the material, is stamped by Add and Update and shown nowhere.
  - **Every tile takes an equal share of the row** (`.figure-tiles`, each tile `flex: 1`), and the made-only tiles are drawn only for a made ingredient, so the others share the row without them.
- **A made ingredient's Total Weight and Total Cost read the form**, so the figures its buffered cost comes from are on screen as they're typed.
  - `BomTotalWeight` is the batch's yield (the recipe's and add-ins' grams less the weight loss, from `IngredientBomRules.CalculateCost`, the formula the cascade saves with), and `BomTotalCost` the lines' cost at their ingredients' buffered costs.
  - They're worked out as they're read (a `Derived<T>`), again after any change to either table, the weight loss (`BomInputsChanged`), new details or a load's merge. Until a picked material's details have arrived they hold what they last were, so the header doesn't show one material's recipe with another's add-ins. Neither is in the snapshot: they're derived, and a recompute would look like a step.
  - **So the Buffered Cost tile follows the form too for a made ingredient**: `BufferedCostText` divides the two, which is what Save then stores, so all three agree while typing. Current Cost stays the saved true cost.
- **The fields row**: Name (a `1.25*` column), Type, Category (also `1.25*`; both `field-roomy`, the rest `*`), then Source for an ingredient or Base Unit for packaging, and Weight Loss % for a made ingredient.
  - **Type and Source can only be picked for a new material** (`CanChangeTypeAndSource`). Switching a new one to Packaging makes it purchased and clears the weight loss and link; switching it back clears the base unit. A packaging material's base unit is fixed once anything is measured in it (`CanChangeBaseUnit`, Domain Model).
  - **Switching an ingredient to Made starts its weight loss at 3%** when the box is empty, so a saved material keeps its own; an emptied box is empty (null), and saves as 0%.
  - **New starts an empty form as a purchased ingredient** (`ClearForm`): no name or category, the cost buffer and target at Settings' default, nothing in the grids. Delete leaves the same form, and saving a new material selects it.
- **The Name field flags a taken name the moment it's left**, not only when Save meets the database's unique index (`Failure`'s unique-violation case, which stays for a race). Leaving the Name box (`TextField`'s `OnLeave`) calls `RawMaterialsState.CheckNameAvailability()`: a case-insensitive, trimmed comparison against the loaded `RawMaterials`, so no round trip, leaving out the material being edited by id. Fixing the name and leaving again clears the message it raised, and no other.
- **The Category picker lists only the categories the material can go in** (`CategoryOptions`, `CategoryTypeFor(EditingType, EditingSource)`, Domain Model), and a new material whose type or source changes under one of another kind lets it go.
- **A name typed into the Category picker that isn't one of those categories brings up a prompt to add it**, so filing a material under a new category doesn't mean a trip to Settings. Products' Category picker works the same way.
  - **When it's asked**: once the picker is left or Enter is pressed in it, through `PickField`'s `OnLeave`, which calls `CommitCategoryTextAsync`. The picker passes on the item highlighted before it says it was left, so a suggestion clicked, or the one Tab or a click elsewhere takes, is the category before the screen reads what was typed (`CategorySearchText`, which `OnTyped` keeps).
  - **What it does** (`CategoryPicker`, shared with Products' picker, through `RawMaterialsState.CommitCategoryTextAsync`): nothing for an empty picker or one showing its own category's name. The listed category of that name, whatever its case, is picked without asking. Otherwise `PromptForNewCategory` opens `PromptHost` titled New Category, with "“X” isn't a category yet. Add it?" over the Name box, filled in and selected. OK saves the category for the material's kind, reloads, picks it and says so in a toast; a name changed in the prompt to a listed one picks that one instead; Cancel, a name the validator refuses, or one saved but not found when read back, puts back the category the picker had.

## The details tabs

- **Cost Setup, Alternative Units, Usage Example, Dependents, then Price History** (`PriceHistoryTab` 4, shown only for a saved purchased material, `ShowPriceHistory`). Dependents sits before Price History so it keeps its place on the strip when that tab hides.
- **The screen state owns the selected tab** (`SelectedDetailsTab`). **Picking a material that isn't purchased while on Price History goes back to Cost Setup, and must do so before that tab hides**: so the `SfTab` bound to it is never left with a hidden tab picked. So the subscription that moves the selection is made before `ShowPriceHistory`'s, and runs first.

### Cost Setup

- **One card by the material's source**: Purchase Cost Setup for a purchased material, saved or new (`ShowPurchaseSetup`); the Recipe card for a made ingredient (`IsEditingMade`); the Linked Cost card for a linked one (`IsEditingLinked`). They follow the form, so a new material shows its card before its first Save.
- **Purchase Cost Setup: the brand grid**, typed into like Alternative Units: Brand, Purchase Unit (a picker over `PurchaseUnitOptions`), Qty per Purchase Unit, Purchase Price, **Unit Price**, read-only: the price per base unit (`BrandPriceRow.CostPerBaseUnit`, to 4 decimals), so brands bought in different units compare side by side, and **Buffer %**, read-only: how far the buffered cost is over that brand's price (`BrandPriceRow.BufferPercent`, `RawMaterialRules.CostBufferPercentOf`), so the brand the cost comes from shows the material's %, a cheaper one more, and one priced above the buffered cost a negative figure. Remove under it acts on the row picked, beside **Basis**, the brand the cost comes from (Application Architecture; the dearest unless one is picked). Application Architecture also has how a unit is added from the grid and what Save sends.
- **The buffer row under it**: Cost Buffer % | Buffered Cost | Per, with two lines of text under them.
  - **The buffered cost is a fixed amount** (Domain Model, "Buffered cost"), `EditingBufferedCost`, per base unit. A cleared box sends 0, which Save works out afresh from the prices and the %.
  - **Typing links the two.** Typing the % (`TypedCostBufferPercent`) sets the buffered cost to the brand the cost comes from, as the grid now is, with the % on top (nothing to work it out from while no brand is priced). Typing the buffered cost sets the % to what it is over that brand.
  - **As prices change the amount stays fixed, and the % follows it.** `UpdatePurchaseCostTexts` sets `EditingCostBufferPercent` to `CostBufferPercentOf(cost, buffered)`, rounded to 4 decimals and clamped to 0–100, whenever it runs with a priced brand and an amount above 0: a price typed, a unit's multiplier, a brand added or removed, Basis, a material loaded, an undo. So the box agrees with that brand's Buffer % column, and the saved `CostBufferPercent` is the live ratio; the % last typed is kept as the target (below).
  - **The % box is capped 0–100** (`MinValue`, `MaxValue`), the range the validator and `CK_RawMaterials_CostBufferPercent` enforce, and the % is clamped wherever it's worked out, so a buffer gone negative or over 100% pins the box. The clamp also keeps the box's own coercion out: a pinned value written back through the two-way binding would reach `TypedCostBufferPercent` and overwrite the amount.
  - **The lines**: `PurchaseBufferedCostText` says what the amount comes to over the brand ("0.0525/g is 5% over the dearest brand's 0.05/g.", naming the Basis brand when one is picked), or, with no amount yet, what Save will work out. `BufferedCostWarningText` shows while that brand costs more than the amount, compared at 4 places, and says to type the % again to put the buffer back on top.
  - **Per is the unit the Buffered Cost is shown and typed in** (`BufferedCostUnitOptions`: the base unit, then the form's alternative units with a multiplier; rebuilt only when they change, and a picked unit that goes falls back to the base unit). It starts on the unit of the brand the cost comes from when a material loads (`DearestBrandUnit`), and isn't saved. The box binds `EditingBufferedCostInUnit`: shown to the centavo, or to 4 decimals on the base unit (`IsBufferedCostOnBaseUnit`, `FormatInBufferedCostUnit`), while what's typed is divided back to the base unit unrounded. So it passes its value on only once it's left (`NumberField`'s `LandsOnLeave`), since the value it shows back is rounded, and takes up to 10 decimals. The lines and the header's cost tiles use the same unit.
  - **Replacing Per's choices is guarded** (`_rebuildingBufferedCostUnits`): a picker bound both ways can write back an empty pick while its list is cleared, and that write rebuilding the choices again halfway through once recursed until the stack overflowed as a material loaded. The tests of the screen's state have no picker to write back, so they can't see it.
  - **Per isn't part of what Save compares** (`RawMaterialFormSnapshot.SavesSameAs`, which leaves `BufferedCostUnitKey` out, as Products leaves its Clone name out): no Save writes it. A unit picked there is still an undo step (`ShowsSameAs` keeps it). A pick shows the base unit in Per until the material's details put its brand's unit there, and the picker's combo box answered that first list late, after the form had settled, putting the base unit back: about one pick in three lit Save with nothing typed. `PickField` takes no value from its combo box while it hasn't the cursor, and the comparison leaves the unit out besides.
  - Only a purchased material's buffer is edited here. A made ingredient adds none of its own, and a linked one inherits its linked-to ingredient's (Domain Model).
- **The target buffer** (`RawMaterial.TargetCostBufferPercent`, 0 to 100, purchased only) is the % last typed: `TypedCostBufferPercent` sets `EditingTargetCostBufferPercent` too, and a new material starts on Settings' default. Every purchased material has one: a save without one stores the buffer (`TargetBuffersUpgrade` gave the older ones theirs). It's in the form's snapshot, so Undo and Save's dirty check see it.
  - While the live % is off it by a tenth of a point or more (`RawMaterialRules.IsOffTarget`, `IsBufferOffTarget`), "Target 10%" (`TargetBufferText`) and **Re-apply Target** show under the buffer row; the button types it again, as one undo step (`ReapplyTargetBufferCommand`).
  - **Reports → Cost Buffers** lists every priced purchased material (`CostBufferRow`) with its buffer, its target and its Brand Cost, "Only below target" by default (`RawMaterialRules.IsBufferBelowTarget`, at one decimal: buffered costs typed to the centavo per bag come out a hair under, 9.98% against 10%). **Re-apply Target Buffer** does it for the rows picked, or all shown below target, once confirmed: `IRawMaterialRepository.ReapplyTargetBuffersAsync` sets each buffered cost to its cost brand plus the target in one transaction and cascades once for the ingredients changed, as a Save does, with no price history entry, since the true cost doesn't move.
  - Tests: `RawMaterialRulesTests`, `TargetCostBufferPersistenceTests` and `RawMaterialsStateUndoTests.Re_apply_Target_…`.
- **The Recipe card** (a made ingredient; `Pages/RawMaterials/BillOfMaterials.razor`):
  - **Made from**: a picker over `Recipe.SubAssemblyOptions` (every recipe on the Sub-Assemblies screen, never a packaging set, read with `LoadCommand`; `Recipe.SubAssembly`). **Once one is picked it shows as a badge**, "Syrup Base, from Sub-Assemblies", with an Edit glyph beside it, as a linked section shows on the Products screen, so it isn't changed by accident. `IsPickingSubAssembly` (nothing picked, or `IsChangingSubAssembly`) swaps the picker for the badge (`IsShowingSubAssemblyBadge`, `SubAssemblyBadgeText`). Edit (`StartChangingSubAssemblyCommand`) brings the picker back with a cancel glyph (`CancelChangingSubAssemblyCommand`), and another pick puts the badge back, as do another material, New and an undo. The change mode isn't in the snapshot; the pick is, so Undo puts the old one back.
  - **The badge opens the sub-assembly** (`OpenSubAssemblyCommand` → `SubAssemblyOpenRequests`, which `ScreenLinks` sends to the Sub-Assemblies screen's address, as it does Products' linked badge).
  - **The recipe's lines, read-only** (`Recipe.Lines`: Ingredient, Grams, Cost/g and Cost), read by `Recipe.LoadLinesAsync` whenever the pick changes, undo included, and after every load, since a load re-points the pick only when its row changed.
  - **Add-Ins**, the ingredient's own extras on top of the recipe, typed into (`Recipe.AddIns`, `SelectedAddIn`, `RemoveAddInCommand`, `NewAddIn`). Its picker offers `AddInOptions`, every ingredient but the one being edited, the same filter as `LinkTargets`, by name alone, as a recipe line's does (the Products notes, Recipe lines); `Ingredients` stays unfiltered, since it also resolves saved lines and links back to their material. Save refuses an add-in that's the ingredient itself even so ("An ingredient can't be an add-in of itself."), for a line saved outside the app.
  - The snapshot carries `SubAssemblyId` and `AddIns`. Save sends `SubAssemblyId` with the material and the add-ins only when they changed (`ChangedAddIns`); a made ingredient with no pick is refused ("Pick the sub-assembly it's made from.").
  - Covered by `RawMaterialsStateRefreshTests.A_picked_sub_assembly_shows_as_a_badge_and_only_Edit_brings_the_picker_back`.
- **The Linked Cost card** (a linked ingredient): Linked To (`LinkTargets`, every ingredient but the one being edited), Weight Share %, Cost Share %, the Cost Buffer % it inherits (a read-only `NumberField`, greyed), a live preview worked out with `RawMaterialRules.LinkedCost` (`LinkedCostPreviewText`), and `LinkedPartsText` naming the linked-to ingredient's other parts and the totals with this one.

### Alternative Units

- **A table typed into** (a `LineGrid`, with its add row): Alternative Unit (a `PickField` over the units of measure, each listed with its abbreviation, setting `UnitOfMeasureId`), Abbreviation (read-only, following the unit) and Multiplier, which can't be typed for a standard unit (Domain Model). Remove, below it, acts on the row picked. A saved unit keeps whatever description it had; nothing shows or edits it.
- **`RawMaterialUnitRow` is an editable row** (an `ObservableRow`) over the form's copy of a `RawMaterialUnit`: setting `UnitOfMeasureId` or `BaseUnitsPerUnit` writes straight through, so Save, the purchase unit choices and the overview, which all read the units, see it. It resolves its unit of measure itself, from a lookup the screen hands it, so the add row names its unit as soon as one is picked; `Units.NewRow` makes the add row's draft (`LineGrid`'s `NewItem`).
  - The screen changes rows in place rather than replacing them under the grid: a base unit change sets each row's `BaseUnitAbbreviation`, a rename in Settings calls `RefreshUnitOfMeasure()`, and a quantity typed in Purchase Cost Setup sets the row's multiplier.
  - An edit in the grid (a row added, its unit or multiplier changed) reprices the brands bought in that unit at once (`RepriceBrands`, from `Units.Rows.ToObservableChangeSet().AutoRefresh(...)`); the screen's own changes go through `Units.Sync`, so they don't count.
- **The Unit Conversion Overview** sits beside it(`AlternativeUnits`): a box per unit pointing down into the base unit, bound to any rows exposing `UnitName` and `BaseEquivalentText`.

### Usage Example

- **A calculator for the material being edited** (`UsageExample`, the page and the part; its `Material` follows the selection): a Use Quantity, a unit and Calculate. The unit starts on the material's largest alternative unit (units are read largest first), else its base unit, and stays as picked through a refresh of the same material.
- **Its big number is the buffered total**, and the breakdown reads quantity in base unit, cost per base unit, calculated cost, buffer and buffered cost, from the material's saved costs, so an unsaved edit on Cost Setup shows only once saved. It's pure arithmetic in the screen's state, over `UnitConversionRules`.

### Dependents

- **The products the material goes into, as tiles that open each on the Products screen** (the shared `DependentTiles`, `Dependents.OpenProductCommand` → `ProductOpenRequests`, which `ScreenLinks` merges with the other screens' and opens at the product's address, `products/{id}`). `IRawMaterialRepository.GetDependentsAsync` counts indirect use too, since a cost change reaches those products just the same: an ingredient through every made ingredient it's in and linked ingredient cut from it, however deep, and every composite holding such a product; packaging through packaging sets and packaging links, down the chain, and composites that include their products' packaging. It's read with the material's other details and again on each `LoadCommand`, so a product changed on another tab shows.
- **Each tile says what the material makes up of the product's material cost**: "38% of materials" under the name (`LinkingProduct.CostShareText`, shown by the shared tile template only when set, so Sub-Assemblies' tiles have none).
  - `MaterialCostShares` (UseCases, through `IRawMaterialQueries.ReadCostSharesAsync`) prices each product as saved with the material at no cost (`IngredientCostRules.Reprice`, then `SavedProductReader.PricedAt` and `Build` with the copies, as the Price Simulator prices a change) against as it is, at buffered costs. Costs move with prices in step, so the difference is exactly what the material brings by any path. A composite is priced with every product it holds.
  - It's worked out only while the tab shows (`Dependents.ShowCostSharesAsync`, through the screen's `LoadLatest`, asked again when the tiles change or the tab shows), since it reads every product the material goes into. `RawMaterialQueries` takes the service as an optional last constructor parameter (none, and no shares show); the tiles' words are `RawMaterialDependents.ShareText`. Tests: `MaterialCostSharesTests` (UseCases.Tests) and `RawMaterialDependentsTests`.

### Price History

- **Its grid fills the tab**: Date, Brand, Unit, Price and Cost / Base Unit, for a saved purchased material. **Delete Price** removes a log entry only (Application Architecture, "Refreshes keep unsaved units"). Save logs an entry only when it changes the material's cost (Domain Model, RawMaterialPriceHistory).

## The line editors' unit pickers

- **Every packaging line editor** (the Products Packaging tab, the Add Sub-Assembly form and a packaging set's lines on Sub-Assemblies) has a Unit picker: a `PickField` over the line's `PackagingUnitChoice`s (`PricedPackagingLine.UnitChoices`), by abbreviation, the base unit first, then the material's alternative units; the line's Unit Cost column shows what one of the unit picked costs.
- **Recipe line editors take grams only**: a sub-assembly's lines and a made ingredient's add-ins show Ingredient and Grams, with read-only Cost/g (the ingredient's buffered cost per gram) and Cost (grams × cost per gram). Recipe editors offer ingredients only, and packaging editors packaging only.
