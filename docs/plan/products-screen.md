# The Products screen

Part of [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md), the project guide: the Products screen as it is now, with every feature it has and the reasons behind them. When each part came in, who asked for it and what it replaced are in the [build plan](build-plan.md)'s dated log. "The app's notes" means [maui-app.md](maui-app.md); a section named without a file (Domain Model, Application Architecture, Reactive Costing Cascade, Conventions) is PLAN.md's. The domain side of what's here (sections, links, packaging, composites, versions, costing defaults) is in the Domain Model; this file is the screen.

**The screen is the Blazor page `Components/Pages/Products`** (since 2026-11-07, when it took the WPF view's place), over the same ViewModel. Its list is a `FolderTree`, its tables typed into `LineGrid`s, its pickers `PickField`s and its boxes `NumberField`s and `TextField`s ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md), "How it's built").

## The screen

- **One screen for products and composite products** (`Products`/`ProductsViewModel`): the list beside the selected product's header and details, in the layout Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies share (the Raw Materials notes, "The screen"), with one Save. With nothing picked, the form is "New product". New names the product in the list and saves it there and then (the list notes, "Names typed in place"); a composite, which can't be saved until it holds two products, keeps its name on the form until Save.
- **The skeleton**(`Pages/Products/Products.razor`; its parts in `Pages/Products/`, each over the screen's ViewModel): `ProductList`; the header card, `ProductHeader`; and under it the details card, an `SfTab` with its tabs along the bottom, bound to `SelectedDetailsTab`: **Recipe** (`RecipeStructure`), **Costing** (`Costing`), **Clone** (drawn in `Products.razor` itself) and **History** (`History`). Clone and History show only for a saved product; the subscription that moves `SelectedDetailsTab` back to Recipe is made before the one that hides them, as Raw Materials does for Price History.
- **The parts that own their work**: the Costing tab is `ProductCosting` (`Costing`), the Packaging tab and a composite's Products table `ProductPackaging` (`Packaging`) and `ProductComponents`, the section form `ProductUsageForm` (`UsageForm`) and the History tab `ProductVersionHistory`. The Packaging tab's commands are its own too (PLAN.md, Conventions: a screen's part owns its tab), each change an undo step it records through the screen, and it asks the screen for what it needs through `IProductPackagingHost`; so is the History tab's Restore, which works out the version (`RestoredVersion`) and hands it to the screen to put into the form (`IProductVersionHistoryHost`). The other parts' commands are still the screen's. The screen keeps what changes the recipe's sections, its tabs and the undo history, and tells each part when what it works from has changed.
- **The header** (`ProductHeader`): the title (the name, or "New product"), with the badges before it (below), then New (a split button: New, and an arrow, a `MenuButton`, offering New product and New composite product), Save, Undo, Redo, Revert, Print, the cost basis button (Unbuffered) and Delete. A divider, then one row of figure tiles, **nothing typed into**: Material Cost, Base Cost (`ProductCostSummary.BaseCost`), Current Price (as typed, "—" until there is one), Markup % and Last Updated ("Sep 15, 2026, 11:44 AM", the tooltip giving it in full when a narrow window trims it). All are per sellable unit.
  - **Markup %** is `ProductCostSummary.CurrentMarkupPercent`, what the current price earns on the base cost with the VAT taken out, "—" with no price. It's amber while it's short of the costing's Markup % (`IsBelowTargetMarkup`, compared at the 2 decimals shown) and red when the price doesn't cover the base cost (`IsCurrentPriceBelowCost`), as the tile's `figure-warning` and `figure-error`; it follows the price and the costing as they're typed.
  - **The badges after the title**: "Priced below cost" while a purchased material in the recipe or packaging has risen past its fixed buffered cost (`BelowCostText`, its tooltip naming them; `RawMaterialRules.IsBufferedBelowCost`; a composite's products' materials aren't in the form, so each says so when opened); "Unbuffered costs" while the screen prices at the true costs (`IsUnbuffered`, below); and while the product isn't saved, where Save will file it (`NewProductTag`, the list notes).
- **The fields typed in** (`ProductHeader`, under the header): Product Name, Category, Quantity Required (a whole-number `NumberField`, at least 1, not drawn for a composite) and Current Price (a `NumberField` bound to `EditingCurrentPrice`, empty rather than 0.00 until typed). New puts the cursor in the list's name box on the new product's row, not in Product Name. A product's description still loads and saves (`EditingDescription`) but isn't shown.
- **The Category picker works as Raw Materials' does**: a name typed into it that isn't a category brings up `PromptHost` to add it (`ProductsViewModel.CommitCategoryTextAsync`, the same `CategoryPicker`). It lists every product category, since there's no type to filter by, and adding one reloads only the categories. Deleting its text clears the category, which is optional for a product. Tests: `ProductCategoryPersistenceTests` and the picker's flows in `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests`.

## The list

- **A tree of folders, as in a file explorer** (`ProductList`, a `FolderTree` over `ProductsViewModel.ProductTree`, `ViewModels/ProductTree.cs`; the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md)) have what the control took, for all three lists). Since 2026-11-12 **the folders are the user's own** (`ProductFolder`, the Domain Model's ProductFolder), not the categories: the user asked for folders "not tied to category", as File Explorer's are, with the category kept on the product to find similar ones by (the category filter, below).
  - **Two stage folders, Production then Innovation** (`Product.Stage`), each holding the user's folders at its top, by name, then the products loose there, by name; each folder its own folders, then its products, the same way, as deep as they go (`ProductTree.Refresh`, which merges the folder rows, `ProductFolderNode.ForFolder`, so a folder renamed, moved or moved to the other stage keeps its row, its selection and whether it's open). **Every folder shows**, an empty one too, so it can be dropped on. A product whose folder the list hasn't been given, or a folder whose parent it hasn't (or one in a loop of parents left outside the app), shows at the top of its stage.
  - **Then the Deleted folder** (`ProductTree.DeletedFolder`; `Stages` is the two stages), bold like them (`ProductTreeNode.IsTopLevel`), with the bin icon (`AppIcon.Delete`). It holds the deleted products straight by name, whatever their stage and category (`ProductsViewModel.DeletedProducts`, filtered by the search, which opens it on a match). A product deleted or restored keeps its leaf, which moves between the folders. **A deleted row is picked as a folder is**: the form stays on what it showed, and `SelectedTreeProducts` leaves it out. It can't be dragged, and nothing is dropped on the folder or a deleted row.
  - **Each folder shows its product count**, muted after its name ("Breads (12)"), the folders in it counted too (a stage's, all of its own), or with a filter on its matches (`ProductFolderNode.ProductCount`). An open folder shows the open-folder icon while it has rows under it (`ProductFolderNode.Icon`: `AppIcon.FolderOpen`).
  - **A ⚠ after a product's name** means its current price earns less than its target markup (`ProductTreeNode.IsBelowTargetMarkup`, from `ProductCostSummary.IsBelowTargetMarkup`), worked out in the background after each load from the products as saved.
  - **A search, or a category picked in the category filter**, shows only the folders holding a match however deep, opened, and they stay open once it's cleared. **The category filter** (`CategoryFilter`, `CategoryFilters`, a closed `ProductCategoryFilter`: All categories, each category, Uncategorized; a `PickField` on a row of its own under the list's toolbar, as wide as the list so "All categories" and a long name show whole, outside the form's undo) shows only that category's products, in their folders, the Deleted folder's included; with the search, a product has to match both (`Matches`). Its choices follow the categories: one renamed in Settings stays picked under its new name, and one deleted puts it back on All. Changing it gives up a name being typed, as a search does; New, New Folder and their "Here" forms clear both, so the new row shows (`ClearListFilters`), and so does a product opened from elsewhere that they hide.
- **Every folder starts open but Deleted, and the ones left closed are remembered** (`Products.ClosedFolders`, by `ProductFolderNode.Key`: "Production", "Innovation", "Folder/12", "Deleted"), through the shared `FolderMemory`, so a folder new since opens too. The category folders' keys kept before folders were the user's own ("Production/12") match nothing now and are forgotten, so the folders the 2026-11-12 upgrade made started open once. Innovation's key kept before it was renamed ("ResearchAndDevelopment") reads as "Innovation" (`ProductFolderNode.CurrentFolderKey`, given to `FolderMemory.Read`); the old key goes once a folder is next opened or closed. While no closed ones are kept, it reads the open folders the list kept before (`Products.OpenFolders`) once. The tree reports each opening and closing (`ExpansionChanged`), which `FolderStateWriter` writes half a second after the last change, off the UI thread, and once more as the screen is disposed; nothing is written until a folder is actually opened or closed, so a screen never shown writes nothing (`CompositionRootTests`, on an in-memory store). It's one person's view, so it's in the view-state file (`JsonViewStateStore`, `%LocalAppData%\TinapayanRMS\view-state.json`), not the database; a missing or broken file reads as nothing kept.
- **Dragging products, or one folder, onto a folder moves them at once, with no Save** (`ProductTree.DropFor`, a closed `TreeDrop`: `Products` runs `MoveProductsCommand`, `IProductRepository.MoveAsync`; `Folder` runs `MoveFolderCommand`, `IProductFolderRepository.MoveAsync`; asked through `IListTreeScreen`, which `ProductList` hands `FolderTree`; a drop made while a move is still saving does nothing). Onto one of the user's folders files them in it (and its stage), onto a stage's folder at the top of that stage, and onto a product in that product's folder (`ProductTree.MoveTo`); the category never changes. Several products picked with Ctrl or Shift move together, in one save (`ProductTree.MovesTo`, leaving out those already there). **A folder is dragged alone** (`ProductsViewModel.CanDrag`: products, or one folder of the user's; a stage and Deleted are fixed), with all it holds, and never inside itself or a folder of its own (`ProductTree.FolderMoveTo`); moved to the other stage, every folder and product in it goes too. A drop that would leave things where they are is refused, and so are products and a folder together. The folder it lands in opens, to show it arriving. The drag is the pointer's (`FolderTree`, `watchTreeDrag`; the app's notes, "How it's built"), and the list scrolls under a pointer held near its top or bottom edge, so a folder far below can be reached (the user, 2026-11-12, with Breads' 53 products between the row and the folder).
- **Cut and Paste move them as a drag does** (the user: "implement cut and paste so that i can move one or more items to another folder"). Cut (Ctrl+X; `CutCommand`) on a product's menu takes the products picked with it, and on a folder's the folder (`CanDrag`'s rule: products, or one folder alone); they're `CutNodes`, faded in the list (`ProductList.RowClass`, `cut`), and nothing is saved. Paste (Ctrl+V; `PasteCommand`) on a stage's folder, one of the user's, or a product (its folder) runs the drop there (`PasteFor`: `ProductTree.DropFor` of the cut rows still listed), with the move's notice and Undo, and forgets the cut; it's greyed where nothing would move. Esc in the list forgets the cut (`ClearCutCommand`, `TreeShortcut("Esc")`). Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V go by the first row picked, whose menu covers the products picked with it, as File Explorer's do; F2 and Del still need one row picked alone.
  - **The notice bar under the tree** says what moved, with Undo (`MoveNoticeBar`, `MoveNotice`, `UndoMoveCommand`, a closed `MoveUndo`): "Moved Pandesal to Production › Breads › Holiday", "Moved 3 products to Production › Breads", "Moved 3 products to Production" (a Promote from several folders), "Moved 3 products", or "Moved Holiday to Innovation › Breads" for a folder. Undo moves each back (another `MoveAsync`), refused with "The folder to move it to no longer exists." when that folder was deleted since (`MoveAsync` checks the folders rather than let the foreign key's refusal read as something else), and takes away the folders a Promote made, while they're still empty (`DeleteIfEmptyAsync`). The bar is Mauve, the neutral notice's colour, as wide as the tree so it wraps, and hides itself 8 seconds on unless the mouse is over it.
  - **One move at a time, whichever kind** (since 2026-11-13, from a cloud review): products moved or promoted, a folder moved and the notice's Undo are each off while any of them is still saving or reading the list again (`_noMoveRunning`, the commands' shared `canExecute`), from a menu, a key, a drop or a paste alike. Each reads the list as it stands and takes the notice's Undo, so a second started meanwhile was judged against the list as it was, and the two took each other's Undo; each command alone had only been off while it ran itself.
  - **A move leaves the form alone**: Save never writes a saved product's stage or folder, and a move never changes the category, so nothing typed into the form, and no undo step, is touched; the form only shows the product's new `UpdatedAt`. (When folders were categories, the Category picker followed a move and every undo step was rewritten.)
- **Right-click menus** (`ProductsViewModel.TreeMenuFor`, `TreeMenuItem`s that the tests check and run as a click would, drawn by `TreeMenu`, a Syncfusion `SfContextMenu`). A row right-clicked outside the selection is picked alone first, as in File Explorer; one inside it keeps the rest.
  - **A product**: Promote to Production or Send Back to Innovation (`MoveToStageCommand`, `IProductRepository.MoveToStageAsync`: to the folder at the same path in the other stage, made if missing; the Domain Model's ProductFolder says why), Move to ▸ a stage ▸ the top of the stage, then its folders, a folder holding others a submenu starting with itself (`MoveToMenu`; the place it's in greyed), Cut and Paste (into its folder), Apply Costing Defaults… (Domain Model, Costing defaults), Rename (F2), Clone…, Create Variant… and Delete (Del). **Several products**: the moves and Apply Costing Defaults, counted ("Promote 2 Products to Production", "Move 3 Products to"), each acting on just the products it would change.
  - **A stage's folder**: New Product Here, New Composite Product Here, New Folder, Paste, Print Stage, Expand All and Collapse All.
  - **One of the user's folders**: New Product Here, New Composite Product Here, New Folder (inside it), Promote All to Production (in Innovation, when it or a folder in it shows products; with a filter on, the matches), Move to ▸ a stage ▸ any place but itself and the folders in it, Cut and Paste, Print Folder (its products and every folder's in it: `PrintScope.Folder.Of`, labelled with its path), Rename (F2), Delete (Del), Expand All and Collapse All (the folders in it).
  - **The empty space**: New Product, New Composite Product, New Folder, Print All Products, Expand All and Collapse All, which are also the toolbar under the search box.
  - **A deleted product**: Restore (puts it back and opens it) and Delete Permanently (Del; asks first, "This can't be undone."). The Deleted folder has no menu.
  - **F2 and Del do what the one row picked's menu shows beside them** (`TreeShortcut`), and nothing with several picked, where a key could take the wrong one; Enter opens or closes a folder; the menu key or Shift+F10 opens the row's menu under it.
- **Names typed in place, as in a file explorer** (`ProductsViewModel.TreeNameEdit`, a `TreeNameEdit` record: the row, the text the box starts with, and its tooltip). Rename (F2, a product's or a folder's), a slow second click on the picked product's name (below), New Folder, and every New turn a row's name into a box (`TreeNameBox`, drawn by `ProductList` in place of the row's title), its text selected. **Enter, or leaving the box with its text changed, saves the name there and then** (`CommitTreeNameAsync`); **Esc gives up** (`CancelTreeName`), and so do an empty name, for a rename the name it had, and leaving the box with the text it started with (`TreeNameBox`), so a click on the form or another row after New saves no "New product". Those rules, and the row named, are `TreeNaming`'s, which the three lists share; what a name does, and what Esc takes away, each screen says. A name refused (one taken: product names are unique, and a folder's beside it) shows why and brings the box back with what was typed. A search typed, or a category picked in the filter, meanwhile gives the box up, since it may hide the row. The prompts these used (`PromptForName`) are left to Clone… and Create Variant….
  - **A slow second click on the product picked renames it, as in File Explorer** (`FolderTree.RenamesOnSecondClick`, products only, as the user chose: a click on a folder still opens or closes it, and a category is renamed with F2 or its menu). It's a plain click anywhere on the row of the one product picked already, not a double-click's second click (`MouseEventArgs.Detail` 2): half a second on (`SecondClickWait`, Windows' double-click time) it does what F2 does, unless another click, a key, a drag or a menu came first. **A double-click renames nothing**, as the user wants. At first only a click on the name text counted, which is short beside the row, so the rename came and went with where the second click landed. A deleted product and the new product's row aren't renamed this way.
  - **The rows being named before they're saved** are `ProductTree.PendingFolder` (`ProductFolderNode.IsNewFolder`, "New folder", after the folders and before the products of the folder it's made in) and `ProductTree.PendingProduct` (`ProductLeafNode.IsNew`, a product with no Id, "New product" or "New composite product", last in its folder). One of each at a time; a load keeps them where they are (`ProductTree.Refresh`), uncounted, and the folders they're in open to show them (`OpenDownTo`). They have no menu, aren't dragged or dropped on, aren't in `SelectedTreeProducts`, and picking the new product's row loads nothing: it's the form's already (`SyncTreeSelection` keeps it picked while nothing saved is).
  - **A new product is saved by its name** (`SaveNewProductAsync`): the name goes into the form outside the undo history, then Save's own path runs (`_saveForm`, what `SaveCommand` runs), so it's saved with the costing defaults and its first version, exactly as one filled in and saved, and opens; opening it takes the row away (`EndNewProductRow`). **A new composite can't be saved until it holds two products** (`CompositeProductRules`), so its name goes into the form as an undo step, Save is offered, and the row keeps the name until Save files it, or Revert or another product takes the form (`NameNewComposite`).
- **New Product Here** (`NewProductInCommand`) starts a new product as New does, with no category, filed in the folder it was asked on and its stage (`NewProductStage`, `NewProductFolderId`, which Revert keeps too), and its row there to be named. New, New Composite Product and their "Here" forms all add the row (`NewProductInList`), clearing the filters first so it shows. **New from the header starts one at the top of Settings' stage for a new product** (`CostingDefaults.NewProductStage`, Production unless set; `_defaultNewProductStage`). While the product isn't saved, a badge before the header's title says where Save will file it, "Goes in Production" or "Goes in Innovation › Breads" (`NewProductTag`). A product started by New (`_newProductInDefaultStage`) follows the setting when a load brings a changed one (`RefreshCostingDefaults`), since its stage was never picked; one started by New Product Here keeps its folder's, and follows the folder when a load finds it renamed or moved to the other stage (`Folders`, which the tag watches, sets `NewProductStage`), as Save files it there (`ProductRepository.AddAsync` takes the folder's stage).
- **Rename saves the name at once** (`RenameProductCommand`, `IProductRepository.RenameAsync`, which renames the product's own sub-assemblies with it, as a Save's rename does). **On the product showing with unsaved edits, only the name is saved, and the rest stays unsaved**: the name box takes the new name, the Clone tab's name follows while it's still "… Copy", and every undo step takes it (`RewriteAll`).
- **Clone… and Create Variant…** ask for the new product's name (starting from "… Copy"), then do what the Clone tab's buttons do: copy the product as saved and show the new one, in Innovation, in the folder at its source's path (`ProductCloner`).
- **New Folder, Rename and Delete on a folder** manage the user's folders (`NewFolderCommand`, `RenameFolderCommand`, `DeleteFolderCommand`; categories are Settings' alone now, and the form's Category picker can still add one). New Folder adds its row inside the folder it was asked on; from the empty space or the toolbar, inside the folder picked, or the picked product's folder, or at the top of Production. The name typed adds the folder (`IProductFolderRepository.AddAsync`), whose row is then picked; a name taken beside it is refused, saying where, and the box comes back. The name box's tooltip says a folder isn't a category (`FolderHint`). Delete asks first, naming what moves up and where ("Delete the 'Holiday' folder? Its 3 products and 1 folder move up to Production › Breads."), and deletes no product (`DeleteAsync`); a new product started in it is filed in its parent instead.
- **Delete** (the header's or the list's) asks "Delete 'Mamon'? It moves to the Deleted folder at the bottom of the list, where it can be restored as it is." A composite's says the products it holds stay.
- **Picking a product's row picks the product** (`SelectedTreeNode`); clicking a folder opens or closes it and leaves the product showing. The row follows `SelectedProduct` however it changes: a link from another screen, a Save, a clone, or New clearing it.
- Tests: `ProductTreeTests` (the nested layout, counts, filters, keys, `DropFor`), `ProductsViewModelTreeTests` (every menu, each item run as the menu runs it, each name typed in place, folder drags, Promote's made folders and their Undo, and the category filter), `ProductFolderRulesTests`, `ProductFolderPersistenceTests`, `ProductStagePersistenceTests`, `DataUpgradesTests` (the first layout), `TreeNameBoxTests`, `FolderTreeTests.A_row_being_named_isnt_dragged_and_the_keys_and_clicks_in_its_box_stay_there` and the two second-click tests beside it, `UndoHistoryTests.RewriteAll_carries_a_change_saved_from_outside_the_form_into_every_step`, `JsonViewStateStoreTests`, the list's flows in `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests`, and `RenameAsync` in `ProductPersistenceTests` and `ProductOwnedSubAssemblyPersistenceTests`.

## The Recipe tab: the Recipe Structure

- **A tab for every section the product uses, its own and linked**, in role order (`UsageEdits` of `ProductUsageEdit`, `RecipeOrder`: the roles' `SortOrder`, a tie broken by saved id, a new one last), then **New Sub-Assembly** while one is being added, then **Packaging**, always there and always last (added and selected once, in `WireRecipeTabLists`), with both its tables there to type into. A composite's **Products** tab comes first (Composite products, below). A new product's Recipe Structure has only Packaging, and opens on it; a blank form opens on its first tab whatever showed before (`ClearDetails` reselects with no previous tab), so a new composite opens on its Products. While Packaging is the only tab (`HasRecipeSections` false), the card shows a caption over the strip saying how to start. **Add Sub-Assembly** and **Delete Sub-Assembly** are buttons in the card's header. A section's batch totals come from its lines (`SubAssemblyRules.CalculateTotals` over the ingredients' buffered costs), so an unsaved one prices like a saved one. What a section, a link, a copy, a move and the add-ins are is in the Domain Model; this is the screen.
- **Tabs are called by their roles** (`ProductUsageEdit.SectionName`: its `RoleName`, or "Filling: Ube" while `IsRoleRepeated`, its `Label` beside the role), linked ones too. `MarkRepeatedRoles` sets the flag on every section whenever `UsageEdits` changes (after `MirrorUsageTabs`) and refreshes the tabs' headers (`SubAssemblyRecipeTab.RefreshHeader`). `DisplayName` gives a linked standalone one's own name where the recipe isn't plain (the add-ins caption, messages). Each tab has its Segoe Fluent glyph, a link for a linked section.
- **`RecipeTabs` is what the recipe's tab strip draws** (`RecipeStructure`'s buttons, `SelectedRecipeTab` the one showing): a `SubAssemblyRecipeTab` for each of `UsageEdits` at the same index, then `AddSubAssemblyTab` and `PackagingTab` (`ViewModels/RecipeTab.cs`). `MirrorUsageTabs` keeps them in step from `UsageEdits.CollectionChanged`, and moves `EditingUsage` onto a section a refresh replaced, or an edit applied after it would add the section a second time. A replaced section keeps its tab, so an edit applied (`ReplaceInRecipeOrder`: replace in place, then move to where it sorts), Make a Copy and a refresh never move the tab showing, and another product's sections take over the tabs index for index (`ReplaceUsages`; Application Architecture, "Picking another record swaps its details in one pass").
- **Showing and leaving tabs.** `SelectedRecipeTab` is the ViewModel's, and `_shownRecipeTab` is the tab whose form is open, so `RecipeTabShown` knows which one a change is leaving.
  - **Showing a section's tab opens the form on it** (`OpenUsageForm`), so a tab is its form and nothing else; showing New Sub-Assembly opens a fresh add form (one already adding is left alone).
  - **Leaving a tab applies the form it was showing** (`CommitUsageFormOnLeave`), so nothing typed is lost. A rule refusing it shows the message and puts the tab back (`_revertingRecipeTab` stops that tab applying its own form again). An edit that changes nothing is skipped (`ProductUsageEdit.ShowsSameAs`), and an add form nothing was entered in just closes (`IsUsageFormUntouched`). `ApplyUsageForm` is synchronous, since a tab change can't await, so a Link applies from `FormLines`, which `LoadUsageSourceAsync` keeps in step with the pick, and a pick whose lines haven't arrived is refused rather than linked as a batch of nothing.
  - **After Save** the form stays open on the section's tab; after Delete Sub-Assembly the next tab shows, else the one before, else Packaging. A reload of the same product (after Save, a new product's first included) shows the same section's tab (by id, or by role and name for one saved since), or Packaging if that showed; **another product's recipe opens on its first tab** (`ClearDetailsForAnother` sets `_isRecipeClearedForAnother`, and `ReplaceUsages` reselects with no previous tab). A new product's first Save isn't another product: `ShowSavedProduct` sets `_detailProductId` before selecting it. `_arrangingRecipeTabs` ignores the strip's own pick while the sections are rebuilt.
  - **Taking New Sub-Assembly's tab away waits for the strip.** The screen hears a `SelectedRecipeTab` change before the strip's binding does, so taking the tab out of `RecipeTabs` then took away the tab the strip still showed, and the strip picked a neighbour of its own. `SelectRecipeTab` sets `_selectingRecipeTab` while its pick is on its way, and its second `RecipeTabShown` takes the tab away; a click, which the strip has already made, takes it away at once (`RemoveAddSubAssemblyTab`).
- **Add Sub-Assembly** is a drop-down of the three ways in, New sub-assembly, Link an existing one and Copy an existing one (`AddUsageCommand` with a `UsageFormMode`; a `MenuButton`). It applies the form showing first, as leaving its tab would (one a rule refuses keeps its tab), then inserts New Sub-Assembly before Packaging and shows it. It stays offered while that tab shows, since that's where the way is changed: another item over an untouched add form only changes its way; over one typed into, it applies what's there and starts another. An add form opened before the roles loaded takes the first role when they arrive, and Save leaves an untouched one alone.
- **Delete Sub-Assembly** (`RemoveUsageCommand`) takes out the section whose tab shows; it's greyed on the Packaging tab, which isn't a sub-assembly. Nothing to confirm: a section goes when the product is saved (Save deletes the product's own; a linked one stays with its owner), and one others link is refused on the spot (`SubAssemblyRules.EnsureNotLinkedElsewhere`).
- **The section form** (`UsageForm` over `ProductUsageForm`) takes the section the way Add Sub-Assembly picked (`UsageFormMode`).
  - **Its top row**: for an add, the **Existing Sub-Assembly** picker for a Link or a Copy (`IsFormPickingSource`), alone on a full-width row. Then Role and Name (optional) for the product's own section (`EditingUsageRole`, `EditingUsageName`; packaging has no name), or the badges for a linked one ("From Cream Loaf", or "Variety Bread Dough, from Sub-Assemblies" for a standalone one, which opens it, `OpenLinkedSourceCommand`, with an Edit glyph that changes the link in place, `StartChangingLinkCommand`), "Also used by Cream Loaf Mini" on one of the product's own that others link, and "Moves to Sub-Assemblies on Save" on one that will. At the row's right end: **Unlink** (`UnlinkIconTemplate`, Segoe Fluent's Cut, since the font has no broken-link glyph) and **Copy** (`MakeCopyCommand`) on a linked section (`IsEditingLinkedUsage`, not `EditingUsage.IsLinked`, which has no value through a null `EditingUsage`); **Move to Sub-Assemblies** and **Move to In-House** on the product's own (`IsEditingOwnedUsage`). The Domain Model has what each does.
  - **Role** offers every recipe role (`FormRoleOptions`, refreshed with `RefreshLinkableOptions`, which puts the pick back since the picker can drop it as its list changes); an add starts on the first role the recipe doesn't have yet, else the first, and a copy takes its source's role and name. Apply refuses sections it can't tell apart (`EnsureSectionsDistinct`, before `SetRecipeAsync`'s own check), only for a section that changed and never for a linked one, whose role and name are its owner's; a name typed alone counts as an entry.
  - **The tables**: the lines (Ingredient, Grams, the percentage, Cost/g, Cost and Base; "Recipe lines", below) with Insert Line and Remove Line, and for a linked recipe the **Add-Ins** table under them (`IsFormShowingAddIns`), with Insert Add-In and Remove Add-In, its figure columns 96px like the lines' so the two line up. A Button's Click fires on release, so `SkipOnClick` doesn't stop them.

  - **The form never takes packaging.** Its roles leave out the Packaging role (`RefreshFormRoleOptions`) and its Link and Copy pickers the packaging sets and other products' packaging (`RefreshLinkableOptions`): the Packaging tab types, links and copies packaging itself (below).

The heading row holds the link's badge, what the lines cost one sellable unit (`ProductPackaging.LinesCost`, after `CostUnitCaption`: the lines alone, since the bulk share has its caption under its own table; it was the product's own packaging cost, which with bulk packaging read as more than the table showed), **Link** and **Copy**, and **Move to Sub-Assemblies** while there are lines of its own (`CanMovePackaging`).

Link offers the Sub-Assemblies screen's packaging sets less the one linked now, and says how to make one when there's none; Copy offers the sets, then every other product (`LinkableSubAssemblyOption.PackagingSourceName`: "Gift Box Set — packaging set", "Cream Loaf — product"). A pick loads the lines (`ApplySourceAsync`, through the screen's `LoadLatest`, so a later pick overtakes one still loading): a set's, or a product's as it shows through any link of its own. Packaging with no lines is refused with a message, the picker left open; otherwise, in one undo step, the picker closes and the lines take the place of the per-piece packaging, linked (read-only) or copied (the product's own). Opening and closing the picker isn't an undo step, and Save doesn't see it. **The picker takes the cursor and opens its list as it shows** (`PickField.OpenOnShow`), so the next key filters it; it waited for a click, and with the Vim keys on, letters typed straight after the press were commands. The picker's width is a flex basis (`.packaging-source`), since Syncfusion sets its width inline, and its list is 420px (`PopupWidth`), as the other name pickers': as wide as the box, about 275px beside a link's badge, it cut every name short. Tests: `PackagingLineRowTests`.

## Recipe lines

- **Lines are typed straight into the table**, in the Products section form, Sub-Assemblies' lines and a made ingredient's add-ins alike: a `LineGrid` (`IngredientLinesGrid`), every cell live at once, its add row at the bottom: a `PickField` on the ingredient, by name, and a `NumberField` on Grams. Remove Line under it takes the line picked, and Insert Line, or the Insert key, puts a blank one in above it. PLAN.md's Application Architecture has what every line table shares.
- **The rows are `IngredientLineRow`s** (`ViewModels/IngredientLineRow.cs`), a `ReactiveObject` with a parameterless constructor for the add row. Applying copies them into `SectionLine`s, so editing the form never touches a section's lines. The picker sets `IngredientId`, and a DynamicData `AutoRefresh` subscription points `Ingredient` at the screen's copy (`IngredientLineRow.Resolve`), which prices the row; it maps by id because a refresh swaps in new ingredient copies. On the Products form the same subscription recomputes the batch, the piece size and the figures.
  - `IngredientLineRow.CompleteLines` checks the rows on Apply and Save: it leaves out a blank row, and refuses grams with no ingredient, an ingredient with no grams, or an ingredient on two rows, each naming it ("Enter how many grams of Cake Flour go into Half Filled, or delete its line.").
- **The Base column** is a checkbox, the last column, with the percentage after Grams ("Baker's %" while a base is ticked, "Absolute %" while none is; Domain Model; `IngredientLinesGrid.PercentHeader`). A linked recipe's table is read-only, so it shows its owner's tick greyed. **One click toggles it**, and `IngredientLineRow.ApplyFormulaBase` pushes the new base to every row as the tick, the grams or the lines change. The add-ins table has the percentage but no tick.
  - Restoring a row (`IngredientLineRow.Restore`) assigns the backing fields, since the `Grams` and `IsFormulaBase` setters clear a typed percentage, and `Percent` refuses one while the base weight is 0, a state a row may be in.
- **The ingredient cell is a `PickField`** over the screen's ingredients (`IngredientLinesGrid`), filtering as it's typed with the best match first and highlighted. **Until the cell has the cursor it's a look-alike of the picker**, as every picker on a table's lines is (`Deferred`, [maui-app.md](maui-app.md)): a recipe's combo boxes, one a line, were most of the time opening or picking a product took. **Its list is the names alone**, 320px wide (the longest ingredient name is 29 characters): until 2026-11-11 each item also showed the ingredient's category and cost per gram, in columns of its own (`ItemTemplate`), which the line shows anyway once the ingredient is picked. Without them the page builds a list of 122 ingredients in about 43 ms instead of 50 (medians, Debug, from the press on the picker's arrow to the items in the page), but it shows at about 75 ms either way, on the same frame in most opens: a first comparison, 60 ms against 72 to 76, was two sessions landing on different frames. Opening a picker reads nothing: the ingredients are read with the screen, so what's left is Syncfusion drawing the list, and a first open after a start, about 140 to 155 ms, warming up. A made ingredient's add-ins on Raw Materials list names alone too. Keyboard entry: type part of a name in the add row (the list opens), Tab to Grams, Enter commits the line and opens the next add row.

## Undo, Save offered, and Revert

Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies use the same pattern with their own snapshots (`RawMaterialFormSnapshot`, `SubAssemblyFormSnapshot`); the developer manual's "Undo" has how a new screen takes it up. Settings stays out: it writes a row on every Save, so it has no unsaved form to put back.

- **Undo and Redo, one finished change at a time, by snapshot and restore, not inverse operations** (`ProductFormSnapshot`, `UndoHistory<TSnapshot>`, `IUndoRecorder`/`IUndoScreen`): the form's state is too mutually derived for inverses to be safe, and a product form is only tens of small objects. `UndoHistory` takes `capture`, `showsSame`, `canRecord` and an optional `savesSame` as delegates, and keeps 50 steps.
  - **A step is bracketed, not diffed after the fact**: `Begin` captures, the gesture runs, `End` captures again and keeps the step only if the two differ, so an abandoned gesture (Esc on the add row, an apply a rule refused) needs no cancel path. Opening a step settles one already open, which is how the add row is covered: its draft joins the lines with no event of its own.
  - **The recording points**:the fields and tables themselves (`TextField`, `NumberField`, `PickField`, `LineGrid`, through `IUndoRecorder`); `Controls/FieldUndoStep` on the fields (keyboard focus in and out, so "typed and left" is the rule and no timer is needed); `AsUndoStep` around the commands that change the form; and `CommitUsageFormOnLeave`, the one path that applies a section. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y reach the screen from anywhere on it (`UndoShortcuts`, the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md))).
  - **The history is built at the top of the constructor, before any subscription**: `WhenAnyValue` fires as it's wired, and the selection subscriptions reach code that marks the blank form clean; a `NullReferenceException` there takes the detail-load subscription down for the session.
- **The restore** (`RestoreSnapshot`) runs under `IsRestoring` (beside `IsRefreshing`, both in `IsBusyApplying`), which the change-sets, the costing recompute and `RecipeTabShown` skip under, so a restore isn't an edit; one `FormLinesChanged()` and recompute pass runs at the end, outside the guard.
  - **It never touches the saved baselines** (`_savedRecipe`, `_savedLines`, `_savedCosting`, `_savedPackagingLinkKey`), so it can't call `ReplaceUsages` or the other `Replace*` methods, each of which resets its own baseline: `ReplaceUsageItems` is the part it can.
  - **Tabs are put back under `_arrangingRecipeTabs`**, so `RecipeTabShown` doesn't apply the form being left again, and `_shownRecipeTab` and `_lastRecipeContentTab` are set by hand: left stale, the next real tab change would apply the wrong form.
  - **Undo puts the form back clean** (PLAN.md, Domain Model, sections: `TryUndo`/`TryRedo` give the settled snapshot as it stood beside the step, and the restore rebases that snapshot's form and tabs onto it, `WithFormOf`). Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies don't rebase.
- **What counts as a step**:
  - **Sections compare price-insensitively** (`SectionsShowSame`): a load landing mid-gesture swaps every section for a repriced copy, and comparing by reference, or through `ProductUsageEdit.ShowsSameAs`, which reads the ingredients' costs, would record a step for a reprice, whose undo would put the stale prices back.
  - **The tab showing is restored but not compared**, so moving between tabs records nothing; undoing a change applied by leaving a tab puts the recipe back and leaves the user where they went.
  - **Leaving a tab applies its form and closes it**, so `CommitUsageFormOnLeave` ends with `UndoHistory.End(rebase)`, measured from its start moved on by `WithFormOf` (what Save compares): only a change to the recipe is a step, while the step kept is the full snapshot.
  - Tests: `Moving_between_the_recipe_tabs_is_not_something_to_undo` and `A_step_left_open_across_a_change_of_tab_records_nothing`, in `ProductsViewModelUndoTests`, and `UndoHistoryTests`.
- **Save is offered only when there's something to save**, on the undo snapshots rather than dirty flags of its own: `UndoHistory` keeps a **settled snapshot** beside its stacks (`MarkClean()` takes the form as it stands, `Clear()` drops it), and `IsDirty` says whether the form still matches. `SaveCommand`'s `canExecute` is `History.WhenAnyValue(h => h.IsDirty)`; Back and Forward and the unsaved-changes question go by it too.
  - **The settled snapshot is taken where the details land** (the end of `LoadProductDetailAsync` and of `StartNewProduct`), never where the header is filled in, or a form measured before the grids arrive reads as changed when they do.
  - **It's re-read on every change** (`RefreshDirty()`, from `this.Changed` and each editable collection), not only when a step settles: a step closes on the way out of a field, and a disabled Save would swallow the click that enables it. The collections are watched through their `PropertyChanged`, not `CollectionChanged`: an `ObservableCollection` enforces its re-entrancy guard only once more than one handler listens, and the recipe's tab mirror changes a collection from inside its own notification.
  - **An edit the form hasn't seen yet keeps Save offered** (`IUndoRecorder.IsEditPending`, set by `GridUndoSteps` around an open cell and by a `NumberField` that `LandsOnLeave`, from the first key typed into it until it's left), since the click that ends the edit is the click that saves. **An add row with something typed into it stays pending until it's committed or dropped** (`GridUndoSteps.StayPendingUntilAdded`, watching the view's `CurrentAddItem`): a press on Save takes the cursor and the cell's renderer ends its edit mid-way through that move, so with the flag cleared there, Save measured an unchanged form and went disabled under the cursor, and the line was never added. Tests: `GridUndoStepsTests`.
  - **Products compares more narrowly than undo**: `ShowsSameAs` includes where the form is pointed, so `SavesSameAs` leaves out the Create Variant name, and `RebaseSettledForm` moves the settled snapshot's form and tab fields onto the form now showing (`WithFormOf`) whenever one opens, closes or is added, never the recipe, packaging, costing or header, so a rebase can't hide a change a Save would write.
  - **A refresh that fills in a form rebases just that field**: Products puts the costing defaults on an untouched new product (`Rebase`), and Sub-Assemblies gives a brand-new form the first role, so the blank form the screen opens with doesn't offer Save. Tests: `Save_is_not_offered_on_the_blank_form_the_screen_opens_with` and `Showing_the_tab_again_is_not_something_to_save`, on all three screens.
- **Revert** (`RevertCommand`, beside Save) puts the form back as last saved, after confirming (`ConfirmDelete`, whose title is "Confirm"): it reads the picked product's detail again (`RevertToSavedAsync`: `LoadIntoForm`, then `LoadProductDetailAsync`), which replaces the recipe, packaging and costing wholesale, so everything staged goes, a section being added or edited included; an unsaved new product goes back to a blank one. **Discarding is all-or-nothing for the whole product**, since the section form has no Apply or Cancel of its own: leaving its tab applies it, and Save does. Test: `Revert_reads_the_product_back_as_it_was_saved_and_drops_a_sub_assembly_being_added`.

## The Costing tab

- **One `Costing` over `ProductCosting`** (the screen's `Costing`): the inputs stacked in the left column, a divider, then the breakdown on the right. It all comes from one `ProductCostSummary`, worked out from the form as it's edited (`RecomputeCosts` gathers the recipe's figures and hands them to `Costing.Recompute`), so unsaved changes show everywhere at once. The tab is drawn over the screen's ViewModel, whose `IUndoRecorder` its boxes take (the page's cascading value), and binds `Costing.…`; the screen marks itself dirty from `Costing.Changed`, and an undo snapshot keeps `Costing.Percentages`.
- **The inputs are tiles**, a coloured edge beside each (Latte Peach, Yellow, Green and Teal down the column): **Labor Metric**, **Overhead Metric** and **Admin Metric** (percentages of the sub-assemblies cost) and **Markup Metric** (of the base cost). Each shows its share of the selling price under it ("11.9% of the price"). **The VAT has no tile**: it's the shop's, set only in Settings → Costing Defaults (Domain Model, Costing defaults), so the tab shows it only as the breakdown's VAT row.
  - **Each metric has a percentage box and an amount box**: a `NumberField` in percent, to one decimal with a "%" after it (`Format="N1"`, `Suffix`: 35 is 35%), and a `NumberField` to two decimals bound to `EditingLaborAmount`, `EditingOverheadAmount`, `EditingAdministrativeAmount` or `EditingMarkupAmount`.
  - **The percentage is the only thing kept**: an amount's getter is the cost it comes to (`Costs.Labor` and so on, raised again on every `Costs` change), and its setter sets the percentage that comes to it, rounded to the costing columns' 4 decimals (`TypedAmount.PercentFor`). Worked out again from that percentage, the amount can come back a hair off what was typed (23.77 as 23.7699…), which the binding would push into the box under the cursor, so `TypedAmount.Shown` hands back the typed amount while the percentage and its cost are the ones it was typed against. With no cost to take a percentage of, the percentage stays and the box goes back to 0.00. Tests: `TypedAmountTests`, `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests.Each_metric_s_value_follows_its_percentage_and_a_value_typed_sets_the_percentage_Save_keeps`.
- **The breakdown**, each row with its amount and % of the price: the sub-assemblies, then each section in recipe order, indented, smaller and muted under their total, named as its tab is (`ProductCostSummary.SubAssemblyLines`); Wastage, labelled with Settings' percentage ("Wastage (3% of sub-assemblies)", read-only); packaging; labor, overhead and administrative (each labelled with its percentage); the subtotal, the base cost, highlighted (`AccentSoftBrush`), where the breakdown ends. A composite's reads its Products (ingredients), with each product under it as "Ensaymada × 6", and "% of products".
- **The price comparison**, a second table under the breakdown, so the suggested price can be read against the store's: Component, then Suggested Price, its heading's tooltip naming the unit (`ProductsViewModel.SuggestedSellingPriceLabel`: "Suggested Selling Price per Piece", "per Unit of 8 Pieces", a composite's "per Unit"), then Current Price (the header's). Both tables' rows are tight (4px above and below; the price row's 5px). Its rows are Markup, one row, each price's amount with its percentage at one decimal, in brackets and the muted ink of the breakdown's % of Price (the store price's amber or red kept), in a narrow column after it with no heading (`.percent`, as narrow as its figure; no column has a set width, the headings and row labels are kept on one line, and the rounding row's label wraps, as the table is about 500px wide on the shop's laptop), so the amounts stay in line down their column (`Markup` and the Markup Metric beside `CurrentMarkup` and `CurrentMarkupPercent`, a dash and an empty percentage with no current price), VAT (`Vat` beside `CurrentVat`) and the selling price, highlighted (`SellingPrice` beside `CurrentPrice`). The store price's VAT is taken out first (`CostingRules.PreVatPriceFrom`, `ProductCostSummary.CurrentPreVatPrice`), as the header's Markup % does. Its Markup, amount and percentage, is amber below the Markup Metric and red below the base cost (`IsBelowTargetMarkup`, `IsCurrentPriceBelowCost`), as the header's are; with no current price its cells are dashes. When Settings sets a price rounding, a last row carries the rounded price in the suggested column (`PriceRoundingLabel`, `RoundedSellingPrice`; Domain Model, Costing defaults). ## One Save writes the whole product

- **Any open form is applied first**, then `ProductValidator` and `CostingParametersValidator` run.
- **The form is compared with what was loaded** (the product's recipe, its packaging and its costing), and `IProductRepository.SaveAsync(product, recipe?, packagingLink?, packagingLines?, costing?)` gets only what changed: the whole `RecipeDraft` (its own sections with their lines, and its links, each with its piece size) or null; the packaging link (`PackagingLinkDraft`, Domain Model) or null; the packaging lines or null; the costing or null.
- **One unit of work** (`VersionedSaves.SaveProductAsync`): the product (added or updated from a copy of its fields, stamping `UpdatedAt`), its recipe, packaging and costing, then its version and the versions of the products sharing what it changed, then the commit (Domain Model, Product versions). Each step goes through the `internal static` write methods the single-purpose repository methods share (`ProductRepository.AddAsync`/`UpdateAsync`, `ProductSubAssemblyUsageRepository.SetRecipeAsync`, `PackagingLineRepository.SetLinesAsync`, `CostingParametersRepository.SetAsync`), so every check runs and a refusal anywhere rolls everything back, sections included. The caller's entities are never attached, so a rolled-back save leaves no id on them. `ProductCloner` adds its product through the same `AddAsync`.
- **Afterwards `LoadCommand` runs and a fresh copy of the product is selected** (`ShowSavedProduct`, with the product and detail the Save read back inside its unit of work), so the form and its baseline are what was saved. `LoadCommand`'s reads go at once on the thread pool.
- **Save finishes its reload before it returns.** Until the reload shows them linked, the form still holds the sections it moved to the Sub-Assemblies screen as new, so a second Save in that moment would keep each twice. The saved detail is handed to the selection's load (`_preloadedDetail`), which shows it without awaiting, so the form closes and opens again in one pass, with no flicker and nothing left to arrive.
- **Linking, copying and Make a Copy of a section, and the packaging link, are edits, saved by Save.** Clone and Create Variant, the list's moves and renames, and Delete act at once.
- Tests: `ProductSavePersistenceTests`, and a `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests` test that fills in a new product and saves it with one Save.

## Print

- **Print on the header** (`PrintCommand`, between Revert and the cost basis button) opens the **Print screen** (`Pages/Print.razor`): the pages as a preview, a sheet of paper for each part that starts a page (`Printing/PrintPane`, `Printing/PrintDocument`), under a bar with the Print picker, the Include choices and Print, which opens Windows' print dialog (`IPagePrinter`). It's always enabled; a click while a picked product's details are on their way does nothing, as Save's does, since binding it to `AreDetailsReady` made it flicker with every pick. The header button is the only way in for one product.
- **One Print screen, following Products.** Its tab opens on the strip when Print is pressed (`ProductsViewModel.PrintRequests`, which `ScreenLinks` turns into its address), and closes with its ✕. Going back to Products from it asks nothing, since it shows Products' form; going anywhere else asks about Products' unsaved changes. It isn't in the menu, since it prints what Products has open. Its load, run on every switch to it as the other tabs' are, asks `ProductsViewModel.PreparePrintout()` (a `PreparedPrintout`: the pages, a `PrintProblem`, or empty) for the product Products has open now, unsaved edits and an open form included, so a product opened since shows here too. While details are still arriving it keeps its pages, and when a rule refuses the form it keeps them and says why in its Include bar, in the warning colour. Back and Forward treat it as a screen (`AppScreen.Print`, no record).
- **What the screen shows, unsaved edits included.** A section form left open is applied first, as Save applies it (`ApplyOpenForm`), as one undo step measured as leaving a tab measures one (`History.End((before, now) => before.WithFormOf(now))`); a rule refusing it shows its message, and the Print tab doesn't open. The footer says "Includes unsaved changes" (`History.IsDirty`), or "Not saved yet" for a product never saved.
- **Both parts in one printout, with tick boxes**: the Include bar's **Recipe** and **Costing** print either part alone, and the one still ticked can't be cleared (each box is disabled while the other is cleared), so something always prints. **The recipe printed without the costing has no costs at all**: the recipe tables drop Cost/g and Cost, the packaging table Unit Cost, Cost and its Total, and each section's piece-size line its cost per piece (`PrintDocument`).
- **What's printed** (`ViewModels/ProductPrintout`, a snapshot taken each time the Print screen is shown):
  - **The title**: the name, then "Category · Stage · Per piece" (or "Per unit of 8 pieces"), then Last updated.
  - **The Recipe**, in recipe order. Each section (`PrintedSection.From`) has its heading, a linked one saying where it's from ("Cream Loaf Dough, from Sub-Assemblies"), then Ingredient, Grams, Baker's % or Absolute %, Cost/g and Cost, an Add-ins row with the add-ins under it, and a Total row (grams, the whole formula's %, the batch cost). Under it, the section's weight per piece, yield and cost per piece, or a note that it has no piece size yet. Percentages read as the form reads them (`ProductUsageForm.Recompute`): against the base's weight while a line is ticked, add-ins included, else against the whole batch.
  - **No Base column**: the recipe tab's ticks stay on the screen (the user's choice), so `PrintedLine` doesn't carry them; the Baker's % header and the percentages still say a base is ticked.
  - **Packaging**: its "Linked to …" note, then Material, Qty, Unit (as typed), Unit Cost, Cost and a Total. A composite's Products table goes where its sections go (`ProductPrintout.Components`, `PrintedComponent`).

  - **The pages are the web view's**: it breaks and numbers them, and `print.css` keeps a heading with what follows it and a table row whole.
  - **Colours** are the screen's palette, with rules dark enough to show on paper (`print.css`).
- **A folder, or all products.** The list's menus have **Print Folder** (one of the user's folders, with every folder in it), **Print Stage** and **Print All Products** (`PrintFolderCommand`, `PrintAllCommand`, `PrintRequests` carrying a `PrintScope`). ## Unbuffered costs

- **The cost basis button on the header** (`ToggleCostBasisCommand`, between Print and Delete) switches every cost on the screen between the materials' `BufferedCostPerBaseUnit` and their true `CostPerBaseUnit`. **It's named for the costs it switches to**: Unbuffered on the buffered costs, Buffered on the true ones (`CostBasisButtonLabel`, `CostBasisButtonTooltip`), and while the true costs show an **Unbuffered costs** badge sits after the title. It isn't a toggle button: a pressed button reading Buffered contradicts itself.
- **For the session, not saved.** `ProductsViewModel.CostBasis` (`Domain/Enums/CostBasis`: `Buffered`, `Unbuffered`) isn't part of the product, its snapshots or `History`, so the switch adds no undo step and doesn't offer Save. It stays from one product to the next and through Revert; every launch starts buffered.
- **Print follows it**, This product and a set alike (`PrintTabViewModel.PrepareSetAsync` reads at the screen's basis), and the page says so: " · Unbuffered costs" after the title's cost caption and in the Costing heading (`ProductPrintout.CostBasis`, `ProductPrintLayout.BasisSuffix`). **Reports stay buffered.**
- **How it reaches every figure**: `RawMaterialRules.CostPerBaseUnitAt(material, basis)` is the one place that picks the cost.
  - The immutable section types hold both: `SectionLine.CostAt`/`CostPerGramAt` and `ProductUsageEdit.TotalCostAt`/`CostPerPieceAt`, worked out in its constructor. So a switch rebuilds nothing, the undo snapshots and saved-versus-edited comparisons are untouched, and `RecomputeCosts` reads the basis.
  - The live rows (`IngredientLineRow`, `PackagingLineRow`) have a settable `CostBasis`, `Buffered` by default, so Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies are unchanged. A `PackagingLineRow` prices its unit choices again and keeps its unit. The screen sets the basis on every row put in the form's tables and the Packaging tab (`OnItemAdded`: a read, an undo, a form opened), in the add rows' factories, and on every row when it's switched.
  - A composite's products: `SavedProductReader` prices each product both ways from the one read (`ComponentCosts`: recipe and packaging, buffered and unbuffered; `ReadComponentCostsAsync`), and `ProductComponents` resolves its rows at the basis, so a switch reads nothing again.
  - The packaging pickers' lists show each material's cost at the screen's basis (`PackagingLinesGrid.Basis`). The ingredient pickers list names alone, so have no cost to switch (Recipe lines, above).

## Composite products

The domain side, what a composite is and how it's priced, is in the Domain Model's Composite products.

- **Making one**: New's arrow offers New composite product (`NewCompositeCommand`), its items flat buttons in a `MenuButton`, which close it on click, as Add Sub-Assembly's do; the list's folders offer New Composite Product Here, and its empty space New Composite Product (`NewCompositeProductInCommand`). `IsEditingComposite` is set with a product's details (`SetComposite`), and a new one's by `StartNewProduct(isComposite)`. The header reads "New composite product", and the costs "Per unit". **Quantity Required isn't drawn** (`IsEditingSingle`), and the fields after it close up.
- **The Products tab** (`ComponentsRecipeTab`, the Composite icon) comes first, before the composite's own sections' tabs: `MirrorUsageTabs` mirrors `UsageEdits` index for index after it (`SectionTabOffset`), and the add tab goes after the sections (`InsertAddSubAssemblyTab`). A composite opens on its Products tab, and deleting its last section falls back to it.
  - **`ComponentsSection` over `ProductComponents`** (the screen's `ComponentsTable`, since an undo snapshot's initializer already names `Components`) is typed straight into, like the Packaging tab: Product (a `PickField` over `ComponentProductOptions`), Qty, Recipe Cost and Cost, with Remove Line, and rows drag into order. Qty is an `int?` in a whole-number `NumberField`.
  - **The rows are `ProductComponentRow`s.** What each product brings is read off the UI thread, once per product, the first time it's picked (`ReadMissingCosts`, so the add row prices itself before it's committed), and again on every `LoadCommand` (`TakeFresh`), so a product repriced elsewhere reaches its lines; `Reprice` prices the lines again when a cost arrives or the basis changes. A reprice isn't an edit: the undo snapshot holds only ids and quantities (`ComponentLineState`, `RecipeTabChoice.Components`). The caption's figure is what the products bring alone (`ComponentsCost`, the Cost column's sum).
  - **The screen watches the rows' `ComponentProductId`, `Quantity`, `RecipeCost` and `PackagingCost` through one `AutoRefreshOnObservable(WhenAnyPropertyChanged(…))`**, not a chained `AutoRefresh` each: each DynamicData `AutoRefresh` takes its own lock, and the subscriber sets the rows' costs, so a cost read landing on a pool thread held one link's lock waiting for another while a load held them the other way round, a deadlock (found in a test run with `dotnet-stack`; the app runs both on the UI thread).
- **Include each product's packaging** is an `SfCheckBox` on Remove Line's row, bound both ways to `IsComponentPackagingTicked`, whose setter makes each change one undo step (`AsUndoStep`) around `EditingIncludesComponentPackaging`, which loading and undo set directly: a step around the box's focus (`FieldUndoStep`) would merge every click made while it kept the cursor. It's in the form snapshot, so Save offers itself and Revert takes it back.
  - **While it's ticked the table shows a Packaging Cost column**, and each line's Cost is Qty × (Recipe Cost + Packaging Cost). The costing still splits them: the Costing tab's product lines take `ProductComponentRow.RecipeCostTotal`, and the composite's packaging takes each row's `PackagingCostTotal`. The rows carry an `IncludesPackaging` flag, which `SetIncludesPackaging` sets on every row and the add row before the change recomputes the costs. The column is drawn only while it's ticked (`ComponentsSection`, `EditingIncludesComponentPackaging`). Print gives its table the same column (`PrintedComponent.PackagingCost`, `ProductPrintLayout.ComponentColumnsWithPackaging`).
  - The caption above the table (`ComponentsCaption`) and the header's Material Cost tooltip say where the products' packaging goes; the metric tooltips follow the kind (`MetricBaseTooltip`, `MaterialCostTooltip`).
- **Add Sub-Assembly and Delete Sub-Assembly** work as on a product, but the form offers a composite only the product-owned roles and Packaging (`RefreshFormRoleOptions`), and to link or copy only sections in those roles, and packaging (`RefreshLinkableOptions`).
- **Elsewhere**: a composite's row in the list has the bag-of-breads picture; Reports' Pieces is blank for one; and its Delete confirmation says the products it holds stay.
