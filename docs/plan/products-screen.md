# The Products screen

Part of [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md), the project guide: the Products screen as it is now, with every feature it has and the reasons behind them. When each part came in, who asked for it and what it replaced are in the [build plan](build-plan.md)'s dated log. "The app's notes" means [maui-app.md](maui-app.md); a section named without a file (Domain Model, Application Architecture, Reactive Costing Cascade, Conventions) is PLAN.md's. The domain side of what's here (sections, links, packaging, composites, versions, costing defaults) is in the Domain Model; this file is the screen.

**Since 2026-11-07 the screen is the Blazor page's** (`Components/Pages/Products`), over the same ViewModel, so what it does, and why, is as these notes say. They were written for the WPF view, the screen's first, so where one names a WPF control or attached behaviour (`SfTreeView`, `SfDataGrid`, `ComboBoxAdv`, a `controls:` behaviour), the Blazor page does that part with the app's own components instead: `FolderTree` for the list, `LineGrid` for a table typed into, `PickField` for a picker, `NumberField` and `TextField` for the boxes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md), "How it's built").

## The screen

- **One screen for products and composite products** (`Products`/`ProductsViewModel`): the list beside the selected product's header and details, in the layout Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies share (the Raw Materials notes, "The screen"), with one Save. With nothing picked, the form is "New product", which can be filled in completely before its first Save.
- **The skeleton**(`Pages/Products/Products.razor`; its parts in `Pages/Products/`, each over the screen's ViewModel): `ProductList`; the header card, held to `HeaderCardHeight`, holding `ProductHeader` and `ProductHeader`; and under it the details card, a `TabControlExt` with its tabs along the bottom, bound to `SelectedDetailsTab`: **Recipe** (`RecipeStructure`), **Costing** (`Costing`), **Clone** (`the Clone tab`, `LinksTab`) and **History** (`History`). Clone and History show only for a saved product; the subscription that moves `SelectedDetailsTab` back to Recipe is made before the one that hides them, as Raw Materials does for Price History.
- **The parts that own their work**: the Costing tab is `ProductCosting` (`Costing`), the Packaging tab and a composite's Products table `ProductPackaging` (`Packaging`) and `ProductComponents`, the section form `ProductUsageForm` (`UsageForm`) and the History tab `ProductVersionHistory`. The screen keeps what changes the recipe's sections, its tabs and the undo history, and tells each part when what it works from has changed.
- **The header** (`ProductHeader`): the title (the name, or "New product"), with the badges after it (below), then New (a `SplitButtonAdv`: New product and New composite product), Save, Undo, Redo, Revert, Print, the cost basis button (Unbuffered) and Delete. A divider, then one row of figure tiles, **nothing typed into**: Material Cost, Base Cost (`ProductCostSummary.BaseCost`), Current Price (as typed, "—" until there is one), Markup % and Last Updated ("Sep 15, 2026, 11:44 AM", the tooltip giving it in full when a narrow window trims it). All are per sellable unit.
  - **Markup %** is `ProductCostSummary.CurrentMarkupPercent`, what the current price earns on the base cost with the VAT taken out, "—" with no price. It's amber while it's short of the costing's Markup % (`IsBelowTargetMarkup`, compared at the 2 decimals shown) and red when the price doesn't cover the base cost (`IsCurrentPriceBelowCost`), through `CurrentMarkupStyle`; it follows the price and the costing as they're typed.
  - **The badges after the title**: "Priced below cost" while a purchased material in the recipe or packaging has risen past its fixed buffered cost (`BelowCostText`, its tooltip naming them; `RawMaterialRules.IsBufferedBelowCost`; a composite's products' materials aren't in the form, so each says so when opened); "Unbuffered costs" while the screen prices at the true costs (`IsUnbuffered`, below); and while the product isn't saved, where Save will file it (`NewProductTag`, the list notes).
- **The fields typed in** (`ProductHeader`, under the header): Product Name, Category, Quantity Required (an `IntegerTextBox`, `MinValue` 1, hidden for a composite) and Current Price (a `CurrencyTextBox` bound to `EditingCurrentPrice`, empty rather than ₱0.00 until typed). `ProductHeader.FocusName()` puts the cursor in Product Name on New. A product's description still loads and saves (`EditingDescription`) but isn't shown.
- **The Category picker works as Raw Materials' does**: a name typed into it that isn't a category brings up `PromptHost` to add it (`ProductsViewModel.CommitCategoryTextAsync`, the same `ICategoryPickerScreen` flow). It lists every product category, since there's no type to filter by, and adding one reloads only the categories. Deleting its text clears the category, which is optional for a product. Tests: `ProductCategoryPersistenceTests` and the picker's flows in `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests`.

## The list

- **A tree of folders** (`ProductList`, an `SfTreeView` over `ProductsViewModel.ProductTree`, `ViewModels/ProductTree.cs`; the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md)) have what the control took, for all three lists).
  - **Two stage folders, Production then Research and Development** (`Product.Stage`), each holding a folder for every product category, by name, then Uncategorized, and each of those its products, by name. **Every category has a folder in each stage**, so an empty one can be dropped on.
  - **Then the Deleted folder** (`ProductTree.DeletedFolder`; `Stages` is the two stages), bold like them (`ProductTreeNode.IsTopLevel`), with the bin icon (`AppIcon.Delete`). It holds the deleted products straight by name, whatever their stage and category (`ProductsViewModel.DeletedProducts`, filtered by the search, which opens it on a match). A product deleted or restored keeps its leaf, which moves between the folders. **A deleted row is picked as a folder is**: the form stays on what it showed, and `SelectedTreeProducts` leaves it out. It can't be dragged, and nothing is dropped on the folder or a deleted row.
  - **Each folder shows its product count**, muted after its name ("Breads (12)"; a stage's, all of its own), or with a search on its matches (`ProductFolderNode.ProductCount`). An open folder shows the open-folder icon while it has rows under it (`ProductFolderNode.Icon`: `AppIcon.FolderOpen`).
  - **A ⚠ after a product's name** means its current price earns less than its target markup (`ProductTreeNode.IsBelowTargetMarkup`, from `ProductCostSummary.IsBelowTargetMarkup`), worked out in the background after each load from the products as saved.
  - **A search** shows only the folders holding a match, opened, and they stay open once it's cleared.
- **Every folder starts open but Deleted, and the ones left closed are remembered** (`Products.ClosedFolders`, by `ProductFolderNode.Key`: "Production", "Production/12", "ResearchAndDevelopment/Uncategorized", "Deleted"), through the shared `FolderMemory`, so a folder new since opens too. While no closed ones are kept, it reads the open folders the list kept before (`Products.OpenFolders`) once. The tree reports each opening and closing (`ExpansionChanged`), which `FolderStateWriter` writes half a second after the last change, off the UI thread, and once more as the screen is disposed; nothing is written until a folder is actually opened or closed, so a screen never shown writes nothing (`CompositionRootTests`, on an in-memory store). It's one person's view, so it's in the view-state file (`JsonViewStateStore`, `%LocalAppData%\TinapayanRMS\view-state.json`), not the database; a missing or broken file reads as nothing kept.
- **Dragging products onto a folder moves them at once, with no Save** (`MoveProductsCommand`, `IProductRepository.MoveAsync`). Onto a category folder or Uncategorized sets the stage and category, onto a stage's folder the stage alone, and onto another product that product's folder (`ProductTree.MoveTo`). Several picked with Ctrl or Shift move together, in one save (`ProductTree.MovesTo`, leaving out those already there). Folders can't be dragged, and a drop that would leave a product where it is is refused. The folder it lands in opens, to show it arriving.
  - **The notice bar under the tree** says what moved, with Undo (`MoveNoticeBar`, `MoveNotice`, `UndoMoveCommand`): "Moved Pandesal to Production › Breads", "Moved 3 products to Production › Breads", "Moved 3 products to Production" (a Promote from several folders) or "Moved 3 products". Undo moves each back (another `MoveAsync`), refused with "The category to move it to no longer exists." when that category was deleted since (`MoveAsync` checks the categories rather than let the foreign key's refusal read as a duplicate name). The bar is Mauve, the neutral notice's colour, as wide as the tree so it wraps, and hides itself 8 seconds on unless the mouse is over it.
  - **On the product the form shows, the Category picker follows a move, and the move is written into every undo step** (`UndoHistory.RewriteAll`), so no undo brings the old category back for a Save to write, and anything else typed stays unsaved.
- **Right-click menus** (`ProductsViewModel.TreeMenuFor`, `TreeMenuItem`s that the tests check and run as a click would, turned into `MenuItem`s by `TreeMenu`). A row right-clicked outside the selection is picked alone first, as in File Explorer; one inside it keeps the rest.
  - **A product**: Promote to Production or Send Back to Research and Development (keeping its category), Move to ▸ a stage ▸ any category or Uncategorized (its own greyed), Apply Costing Defaults… (Domain Model, Costing defaults), Rename… (F2), Clone…, Create Variant… and Delete (Del). **Several products**: the moves and Apply Costing Defaults, counted ("Promote 2 Products to Production", "Move 3 Products to"), each acting on just the products it would change.
  - **A stage's folder**: New Product Here, New Composite Product Here, New Category…, Print Stage, Expand All and Collapse All.
  - **A category's folder**: New Product Here, New Composite Product Here, Promote All to Production (in Research and Development, when it shows products; with a search on, the matches), Print Folder, New Category…, Rename Category… (F2) and Delete Category (Del). **Uncategorized**: the same without the category items.
  - **The empty space**: New Product, New Composite Product, New Category…, Print All Products, Expand All and Collapse All, which are also the toolbar under the search box.
  - **A deleted product**: Restore (puts it back and opens it) and Delete Permanently (Del; asks first, "This can't be undone."). The Deleted folder has no menu.
  - **F2 and Del do what the one row picked's menu shows beside them** (`TreeShortcut`), and nothing with several picked, where a key could take the wrong one; Enter opens or closes a folder; the menu key or Shift+F10 opens the row's menu under it.
- **New Product Here** (`NewProductInCommand`) starts a new product as New does, with the Category picker on the folder's category and the folder's stage kept for Save (`NewProductStage`, which Revert keeps too); the cursor goes to Name. **New from the header starts one with no category, in Settings' stage for a new product** (`CostingDefaults.NewProductStage`, Production unless set; `_defaultNewProductStage`). While the product isn't saved, a tag after the header's title says where Save will file it, "Goes in Production" or "Goes in Research and Development" (`NewProductTag`, in `LinkedBadgeStyle`), docked so a long name trims with the tag still showing. A product started by New (`_newProductInDefaultStage`) follows the setting when a load brings a changed one (`RefreshCostingDefaults`), since its stage was never picked; one started by New Product Here keeps its folder's.
- **Rename… saves the name at once** (`RenameProductCommand`, `IProductRepository.RenameAsync`, which renames the product's own sub-assemblies with it, as a Save's rename does). **On the product showing with unsaved edits, only the name is saved** (or, for a move, the stage and category), **and the rest stays unsaved**: the name box takes the new name, the Clone tab's name follows while it's still "… Copy", and every undo step takes it (`RewriteAll`).
- **Clone… and Create Variant…** ask for the new product's name (starting from "… Copy"), then do what the Clone tab's buttons do: copy the product as saved and show the new one.
- **New Category…, Rename Category… and Delete Category** do in the list what Settings' Product Categories tab does; a category is a folder in both stages, which the prompts say (`PromptForName`, a `PromptHost`). A new one's folder is picked in the stage it was asked in (a name already listed picks that one's folder); a rename keeps the Category picker on it; Delete Category asks first, naming how many products it leaves uncategorized, then reloads and rewrites the form's undo steps so none puts back a category that no longer exists.
- **Delete** (the header's or the list's) asks "Delete 'Mamon'? It moves to the Deleted folder at the bottom of the list, where it can be restored as it is." A composite's says the products it holds stay.
- **Picking a product's row picks the product** (`SelectedTreeNode`); clicking a folder opens or closes it and leaves the product showing. The row follows `SelectedProduct` however it changes: a link from another screen, a Save, a clone, or New clearing it.
- Tests: `ProductTreeTests`, `ProductsViewModelTreeTests` (every menu, and each item run as the menu runs it), `ProductStagePersistenceTests`, `UndoHistoryTests.RewriteAll_carries_a_change_saved_from_outside_the_form_into_every_step`, `JsonViewStateStoreTests`, the list's flows in `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests`, and `RenameAsync` in `ProductPersistenceTests` and `ProductOwnedSubAssemblyPersistenceTests`.

## The Recipe tab: the Recipe Structure

- **A tab for every section the product uses, its own and linked**, in role order (`UsageEdits` of `ProductUsageEdit`, `RecipeOrder`: the roles' `SortOrder`, a tie broken by saved id, a new one last), then **New Sub-Assembly** while one is being added, then **Packaging** while the product has any. A composite's **Products** tab comes first (Composite products, below). A new product's Recipe Structure is empty (`HasRecipeTabs` false), and the card shows a caption saying how to start in place of the strip. **Add Sub-Assembly** and **Delete Sub-Assembly** are buttons in the card's header. A section's batch totals come from its lines (`SubAssemblyRules.CalculateTotals` over the ingredients' buffered costs), so an unsaved one prices like a saved one. What a section, a link, a copy, a move and the add-ins are is in the Domain Model; this is the screen.
- **Tabs are called by their roles** (`ProductUsageEdit.SectionName`: its `RoleName`, or "Filling: Ube" while `IsRoleRepeated`, its `Label` beside the role), linked ones too. `MarkRepeatedRoles` sets the flag on every section whenever `UsageEdits` changes (after `MirrorUsageTabs`) and refreshes the tabs' headers (`SubAssemblyRecipeTab.RefreshHeader`). `DisplayName` gives a linked standalone one's own name where the recipe isn't plain (the add-ins caption, messages). Each tab has its Segoe Fluent glyph, a link for a linked section.
- **`RecipeTabs` is what the `TabControlExt` binds**: a `SubAssemblyRecipeTab` for each of `UsageEdits` at the same index, then `AddSubAssemblyTab` and `PackagingTab` (`ViewModels/RecipeTab.cs`). `MirrorUsageTabs` keeps them in step from `UsageEdits.CollectionChanged`, and moves `EditingUsage` onto a section a refresh replaced, or an edit applied after it would add the section a second time. A replaced section keeps its tab, so an edit applied (`ReplaceInRecipeOrder`: replace in place, then move to where it sorts), Make a Copy and a refresh never move the tab showing, and another product's sections take over the tabs index for index (`ReplaceUsages`; Application Architecture, "Picking another record swaps its details in one pass").
- **Showing and leaving tabs.** `SelectedRecipeTab` is the ViewModel's, and `_shownRecipeTab` is the tab whose form is open, so `RecipeTabShown` knows which one a change is leaving.
  - **Showing a section's tab opens the form on it** (`OpenUsageForm`), so a tab is its form and nothing else; showing New Sub-Assembly opens a fresh add form (one already adding is left alone).
  - **Leaving a tab applies the form it was showing** (`CommitUsageFormOnLeave`), so nothing typed is lost. A rule refusing it shows the message and puts the tab back (`_revertingRecipeTab` stops that tab applying its own form again). An edit that changes nothing is skipped (`ProductUsageEdit.ShowsSameAs`), and an add form nothing was entered in just closes (`IsUsageFormUntouched`). `ApplyUsageForm` is synchronous, since a tab change can't await, so a Link applies from `FormLines`, which `LoadUsageSourceAsync` keeps in step with the pick, and a pick whose lines haven't arrived is refused rather than linked as a batch of nothing.
  - **After Save** the form stays open on the section's tab; after Delete Sub-Assembly the next tab shows, else the one before, else Packaging. A reload of the same product (after Save, a new product's first included) shows the same section's tab (by id, or by role and name for one saved since), or Packaging if that showed; **another product's recipe opens on its first tab** (`ClearDetailsForAnother` sets `_isRecipeClearedForAnother`, and `ReplaceUsages` reselects with no previous tab). A new product's first Save isn't another product: `ShowSavedProduct` sets `_detailProductId` before selecting it. `_arrangingRecipeTabs` ignores the strip's own pick while the sections are rebuilt.
  - **Taking New Sub-Assembly's tab away waits for the strip.** The screen hears a `SelectedRecipeTab` change before the strip's binding does, so taking the tab out of `RecipeTabs` then took away the tab the strip still showed, and the strip picked a neighbour of its own. `SelectRecipeTab` sets `_selectingRecipeTab` while its pick is on its way, and its second `RecipeTabShown` takes the tab away; a click, which the strip has already made, takes it away at once (`RemoveAddSubAssemblyTab`).
- **Add Sub-Assembly** is a drop-down of the three ways in, New sub-assembly, Link an existing one and Copy an existing one (`AddUsageCommand` with a `UsageFormMode`; the `DropDownButtonAdv` in the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md))). It applies the form showing first, as leaving its tab would (one a rule refuses keeps its tab), then inserts New Sub-Assembly before Packaging and shows it. It stays offered while that tab shows, since that's where the way is changed: another item over an untouched add form only changes its way; over one typed into, it applies what's there and starts another. An add form opened before the roles loaded takes the first role when they arrive, and Save leaves an untouched one alone.
- **Delete Sub-Assembly** (`RemoveUsageCommand`) takes out the section whose tab shows, or on the Packaging tab its lines or link (`RemovePackaging`). Nothing to confirm: a section goes when the product is saved (Save deletes the product's own; a linked one stays with its owner), and one others link is refused on the spot (`SubAssemblyRules.EnsureNotLinkedElsewhere`).
- **The section form** (`UsageForm` over `ProductUsageForm`) takes the section the way Add Sub-Assembly picked (`UsageFormMode`).
  - **Its top row**: for an add, the **Existing Sub-Assembly or Packaging** picker for a Link or a Copy (`IsFormPickingSource`), alone on a full-width row. Then Role and Name (optional) for the product's own section (`EditingUsageRole`, `EditingUsageName`; packaging has no name), or the badges for a linked one ("From Cream Loaf", or "Variety Bread Dough, from Sub-Assemblies" for a standalone one, which opens it, `OpenLinkedSourceCommand`, with an Edit glyph that changes the link in place, `StartChangingLinkCommand`), "Also used by Cream Loaf Mini" on one of the product's own that others link, and "Moves to Sub-Assemblies on Save" on one that will. At the row's right end: **Unlink** (`UnlinkIconTemplate`, Segoe Fluent's Cut, since the font has no broken-link glyph) and **Copy** (`MakeCopyCommand`) on a linked section (`IsEditingLinkedUsage`, not `EditingUsage.IsLinked`, which has no value through a null `EditingUsage`); **Move to Sub-Assemblies** and **Move to In-House** on the product's own (`IsEditingOwnedUsage`). The Domain Model has what each does.
  - **Role** offers every recipe role (`FormRoleOptions`, refreshed with `RefreshLinkableOptions`, which puts the pick back since the picker can drop it as its list changes); an add starts on the first role the recipe doesn't have yet, else the first, and a copy takes its source's role and name. **The pickers' `IsEditable` and `AutoCompleteMode` are spelled out in XAML**, since the form sits in a collapsed panel whose pickers never raise `Loaded` (the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md)), Pickers). Apply refuses sections it can't tell apart (`EnsureSectionsDistinct`, before `SetRecipeAsync`'s own check), only for a section that changed and never for a linked one, whose role and name are its owner's; a name typed alone counts as an entry.
  - **The tables**: the lines (Ingredient, Grams, the percentage, Cost/g, Cost and Base; "Recipe lines", below) with Insert Line and Remove Line, and for a linked recipe the **Add-Ins** table under them (`IsFormShowingAddIns`), with Insert Add-In and Remove Add-In, its figure columns 96px like the lines' so the two line up. A linked section's lines show read-only: `FormLines` is filled for every mode, `IsFormShowingLines` shows the table and `IsFormEditingLines` alone drives `AllowEditing`, `AllowDeleting`, Role and Name and the line buttons, and the add row is hidden too (`AddNewRowPositionConverter`), since `AllowEditing="False"` would leave it inviting a line the grid then refuses.
  - **The add-ins badge**, level with the linked badge at the lines table's top right corner (a background-less layer over that row, the piece-size column's width shared through `SharedSizeGroup="PieceSizeColumn"`): "No add-ins", "1 add-in", "3 add-ins" (`FormAddInsBadgeText`; `FormAddInCount` counts lines with an ingredient, so not the add row). With add-ins it's a link (`AddInsBadge_Click`) that focuses the add-ins grid and brings it into view; under the Add-Ins heading a **Go to top** badge (`GoToTopBadge_Click`) focuses the lines grid and brings the form's top left corner into view. A Button's Click fires on release, so `SkipOnClick` doesn't stop them.
  - **The summary column**, 230px right of the tables: two tiles, **Cost/piece** (`UsageFormCostPerPieceText`) and **Batch cost** (`UsageFormBatchCostText`, the lines and add-ins), then "Batch parameters", ruled label-left rows of **Weight/piece (g)** and **Yield (pcs)**, then "Computed", **Total weight** as plain text, and a caption saying which figure stays put. Typing either of the pair works out the other live, rounded to the 2 decimals shown, and records which was typed (`AuthoredField`): the boxes' setters are the typing (`PieceSizeTyped`), and the screen shows a size (`ShowPieceSize`) or follows the batch (`FollowPieceSize`, over `ProductRules.ResolvePieceSize`) through methods of their own, so nothing has to tell the two apart.
  - **Packaging comes in through the same form.** New and Copy offer the Packaging role, and Link and Copy packaging sources ("Gift Box Set — Sub-Assemblies (Packaging)" for a set, "Packaging — Cream Loaf" for another product's; `LinkableSubAssemblyOption` is a sub-assembly or a product, keyed by `Key`), both only while the product has no packaging (`HasPackaging`). With either (`IsFormPackaging`) the form swaps its ingredients, add-ins and piece size for a packaging table (`FormPackagingLines`, read-only for a link), and applying it (`ApplyPackagingForm`, through `ApplyForm`) fills the Packaging tab's lines, sets or clears the link, and adds the Packaging tab, always last (`SetHasPackaging`). With no tabs nothing is selected (`PackagingTabIfShown`). Tests: `ProductsViewModelPackagingTests`.
- **The Packaging tab** (`PackagingSection` over `ProductPackaging`) is typed straight into, a `LineGrid` like the form's lines (`PackagingLinesGrid`): the add row at the bottom (its draft from `NewLineEdit`), a `PickField` for the material, and Remove Line, with the cost per piece beside it. Linked packaging shows read-only (`IsPackagingEditable` turns off editing, deleting and the add row, and hides Remove Line).
  - **Columns: Material, Qty, Unit, Unit Cost and Cost per Piece.** Qty is typed in whichever of the material's units the Unit cell names, its base unit or an alternative unit, which `PackagingLineRow` keeps as `UnitId`. What it comes to in base units is never shown, though `PackagingLineRow.BaseQty` works it out for the line's cost. A line saved as pieces per unit shows as the quantity it comes to (`ReconcileReciprocal`), and is rewritten that way when the packaging is next saved.
  - **`PackagingLineRow.UnitCost` is the picked unit's cost, and must not feed the line's total**: `ProductRules.CalculatePackagingLineCost` multiplies its first argument by the quantity in base units, so a figure already scaled to the unit would be converted twice (a ₱50 roll would cost ₱50,000). The row keeps a private `CostPerBaseUnit` for that call. **A row is never in a unit its material doesn't have**: `UnitKey`'s setter falls back to the base unit for a key its `UnitOptions` don't offer, so a material changed, an undo or a snapshot can't leave one that Save would refuse. Tests: `PackagingLineRowTests`.
  - **The rows are `PackagingLineRow`s** (`ViewModels/PackagingLineRow.cs`), built like `IngredientLineRow`: the picker sets `PackagingMaterialId`, and the screen points `PackagingMaterial` at its copy (`Resolve`, also on refresh, in place), which prices the row. `PackagingLineRow.CompleteLines` checks them on Save: blank rows dropped; a material without a quantity, a quantity without a material, or a material on two rows refused.

## Recipe lines

- **Lines are typed straight into the table**, in the Products section form, Sub-Assemblies' lines and a made ingredient's add-ins alike: an editable `SfDataGrid` with `AddNewRowPosition="Bottom"`, `EditTrigger="OnTap"`, `AllowDeleting` (Delete removes the line picked; Remove Line stays under it), a `GridMultiColumnDropDownList` on `IngredientId` and a `NumericColumn` on `Grams`. PLAN.md's Application Architecture has what every line grid shares: the drawn filler rows, row headers, dragging lines into order, Insert, a drop-down cell writing on commit, and Tab from the add row.
- **The rows are `IngredientLineRow`s** (`ViewModels/IngredientLineRow.cs`), a `ReactiveObject` with a parameterless constructor for the add row. Applying copies them into `SectionLine`s, so editing the form never touches a section's lines. The picker sets `IngredientId`, and a DynamicData `AutoRefresh` subscription points `Ingredient` at the screen's copy (`IngredientLineRow.Resolve`), which prices the row; it maps by id because a refresh swaps in new ingredient copies. On the Products form the same subscription recomputes the batch, the piece size and the figures.
  - `IngredientLineRow.CompleteLines` checks the rows on Apply and Save: it leaves out a blank row, and refuses grams with no ingredient, an ingredient with no grams, or an ingredient on two rows, each naming it ("Enter how many grams of Cake Flour go into Half Filled, or delete its line.").
- **The Base column** is a `GridCheckBoxColumn`, the last column, with the percentage after Grams ("Baker's %" while a base is ticked, "Absolute %" while none is; Domain Model). Its header is a `HeaderTemplate` binding the view's `DataContext` (`PercentHeaderTemplate`), since a changed `HeaderText` reaches the header cell only through the row generator, which doesn't redraw the header of a grid a newly shown tab brings in. On Products the column's `AllowEditing` binds `IsFormEditingLines`, so a linked recipe shows its owner's tick greyed. **One click toggles it** (with `EditTrigger="OnTap"`), and `IngredientLineRow.ApplyFormulaBase` pushes the new base to every row as the tick, the grams or the lines change. The add-ins table has the percentage but no tick.
  - Restoring a row (`IngredientLineRow.Restore`) assigns the backing fields, since the `Grams` and `IsFormulaBase` setters clear a typed percentage, and `Percent` refuses one while the base weight is 0, a state a row may be in.
- **The ingredient cell autocompletes**: `DisplayMember="Name"`, `ValueMember="Id"`, the screen's ingredients as `ItemsSource`, `AllowAutoComplete` picking as it's typed and `AllowIncrementalFiltering` narrowing the drop-down, `SearchCondition="Contains"`. The drop-down is a small grid of Name, Category and Cost/g (the buffered cost, 4 decimals); it needs `IsAutoPopupSize="False"` with a `PopUpWidth` and `PopUpHeight`, or it takes the cell's width and clips the cost, and `GridColumnSizer="Star"`, so Name takes what the fixed columns leave. Keyboard entry: type part of a name in the add row (the drop-down opens), Tab to Grams, Enter commits the line and opens the next add row.

## Undo, Save offered, and Revert

Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies use the same pattern with their own snapshots (`RawMaterialFormSnapshot`, `SubAssemblyFormSnapshot`); the developer manual's "Undo" has how a new screen takes it up. Settings stays out: it writes a row on every Save, so it has no unsaved form to put back.

- **Undo and Redo, one finished change at a time, by snapshot and restore, not inverse operations** (`ProductFormSnapshot`, `UndoHistory<TSnapshot>`, `IUndoRecorder`/`IUndoScreen`): the form's state is too mutually derived for inverses to be safe, and a product form is only tens of small objects. `UndoHistory` takes `capture`, `showsSame`, `canRecord` and an optional `savesSame` as delegates, and keeps 50 steps.
  - **A step is bracketed, not diffed after the fact**: `Begin` captures, the gesture runs, `End` captures again and keeps the step only if the two differ, so an abandoned gesture (Esc on the add row, an apply a rule refused) needs no cancel path. Opening a step settles one already open, which is how the add row is covered: `SfDataGrid` raises nothing when an add commits.
  - **The recording points**:the fields and tables themselves (`TextField`, `NumberField`, `PickField`, `LineGrid`, through `IUndoRecorder`); `Controls/FieldUndoStep` on the fields (keyboard focus in and out, so "typed and left" is the rule and no timer is needed); `AsUndoStep` around the commands that change the form; and `CommitUsageFormOnLeave`, the one path that applies a section. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y reach the screen from anywhere on it (`UndoShortcuts`, the app's notes ([maui-app.md](maui-app.md))).
  - **The history is built at the top of the constructor, before any subscription**: `WhenAnyValue` fires as it's wired, and the selection subscriptions reach code that marks the blank form clean; a `NullReferenceException` there takes the detail-load subscription down for the session.
- **The restore** (`RestoreSnapshot`) runs under `IsRestoring` (beside `IsRefreshing`, both in `IsBusyApplying`), which the change-sets, the costing recompute and `RecipeTabShown` skip under, so a restore isn't an edit; one `FormLinesChanged()` and recompute pass runs at the end, outside the guard.
  - **It never touches the saved baselines** (`_savedRecipe`, `_savedLines`, `_savedCosting`, `_savedPackagingLinkKey`), so it can't call `ReplaceUsages` or the other `Replace*` methods, each of which resets its own baseline: `ReplaceUsageItems` is the part it can.
  - **Tabs are put back under `_arrangingRecipeTabs`**, so `RecipeTabShown` doesn't apply the form being left again, and `_shownRecipeTab` and `_lastRecipeContentTab` are set by hand: left stale, the next real tab change would apply the wrong form.
  - **Undo puts the form back clean** (PLAN.md, Domain Model, sections: `TryUndo`/`TryRedo` give the settled snapshot as it stood beside the step, and the restore rebases that snapshot's form and tabs onto it, `WithFormOf`). Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies don't rebase.
- **What counts as a step**:
  - **Sections compare price-insensitively** (`SectionsShowSame`): a load landing mid-gesture swaps every section for a repriced copy, and comparing by reference, or through `ProductUsageEdit.ShowsSameAs`, which reads the ingredients' costs, would record a step for a reprice, whose undo would put the stale prices back.
  - **The tab showing is restored but not compared**, so moving between tabs records nothing; undoing a change applied by leaving a tab puts the recipe back and leaves the user where they went.
  - **Leaving a tab applies its form and closes it**, so `CommitUsageFormOnLeave` ends with `UndoHistory.End(rebase)`, measured from its start moved on by `WithFormOf` (what Save compares): only a change to the recipe is a step, while the step kept is the full snapshot.
  - Tests: `Moving_between_the_recipe_tabs_is_not_something_to_undo` and `A_step_left_open_across_a_change_of_tab_records_nothing`, in `ProductsViewModelUndoTests`, and `UndoHistoryTests`.
- **Save is offered only when there's something to save**, on the undo snapshots rather than dirty flags of its own: `UndoHistory` keeps a **settled snapshot** beside its stacks (`MarkClean()` takes the form as it stands, `Clear()` drops it), and `IsDirty` says whether the form still matches. `SaveCommand`'s `canExecute` is `History.WhenAnyValue(h => h.IsDirty)`; Back and Forward and the unsaved-changes question go by it too.
  - **The settled snapshot is taken where the details land** (the end of `LoadProductDetailAsync` and of `StartNewProduct`), never where the header is filled in, or a form measured before the grids arrive reads as changed when they do.
  - **It's re-read on every change** (`RefreshDirty()`, from `this.Changed` and each editable collection), not only when a step settles: a step closes on the way out of a field, and a disabled Save would swallow the click that enables it. The collections are watched through their `PropertyChanged`, not `CollectionChanged`: an `ObservableCollection` enforces its re-entrancy guard only once more than one handler listens, and the recipe's tab mirror changes a collection from inside its own notification.
  - **An edit the form hasn't seen yet keeps Save offered** (`IUndoRecorder.IsEditPending`, set by `GridUndoSteps` around an open cell and by `FieldUndoStep.LandsOnLeave` on a box bound `UpdateSourceTrigger=LostFocus`), since the click that ends the edit is the click that saves. **An add row with something typed into it stays pending until it's committed or dropped** (`GridUndoSteps.StayPendingUntilAdded`, watching the view's `CurrentAddItem`): a press on Save takes the cursor and the cell's renderer ends its edit mid-way through that move, so with the flag cleared there, Save measured an unchanged form and went disabled under the cursor, and the line was never added. Tests: `GridUndoStepsTests`.
  - **Products compares more narrowly than undo**: `ShowsSameAs` includes where the form is pointed, so `SavesSameAs` leaves out the Create Variant name, and `RebaseSettledForm` moves the settled snapshot's form and tab fields onto the form now showing (`WithFormOf`) whenever one opens, closes or is added, never the recipe, packaging, costing or header, so a rebase can't hide a change a Save would write.
  - **A refresh that fills in a form rebases just that field**: Products puts the costing defaults on an untouched new product (`Rebase`), and Sub-Assemblies gives a brand-new form the first role, so the blank form the screen opens with doesn't offer Save. Tests: `Save_is_not_offered_on_the_blank_form_the_screen_opens_with` and `Showing_the_tab_again_is_not_something_to_save`, on all three screens.
- **Revert** (`RevertCommand`, beside Save) puts the form back as last saved, after confirming (`ConfirmDelete`, whose title is "Confirm"): it reads the picked product's detail again (`RevertToSavedAsync`: `LoadIntoForm`, then `LoadProductDetailAsync`), which replaces the recipe, packaging and costing wholesale, so everything staged goes, a section being added or edited included; an unsaved new product goes back to a blank one. **Discarding is all-or-nothing for the whole product**, since the section form has no Apply or Cancel of its own: leaving its tab applies it, and Save does. Test: `Revert_reads_the_product_back_as_it_was_saved_and_drops_a_sub_assembly_being_added`.

## The Costing tab

- **One `Costing` over `ProductCosting`** (the screen's `Costing`): the inputs stacked in the left column, a divider, then the breakdown on the right. It all comes from one `ProductCostSummary`, worked out from the form as it's edited (`RecomputeCosts` gathers the recipe's figures and hands them to `Costing.Recompute`), so unsaved changes show everywhere at once. The card keeps the screen as its `DataContext`, since `FieldUndoStep.Recorder="{Binding}"` needs the screen's `IUndoRecorder`, and binds `Costing.…`; the screen marks itself dirty from `Costing.Changed`, and an undo snapshot keeps `Costing.Percentages`.
- **The inputs are tiles**, a coloured edge beside each (Latte Peach, Yellow, Green and Teal down the column): **Labor Metric**, **Overhead Metric** and **Admin Metric** (percentages of the sub-assemblies cost) and **Markup Metric** (of the base cost). Each shows its share of the selling price under it ("11.9% of the price"). **The VAT has no tile**: it's the shop's, set only in Settings → Costing Defaults (Domain Model, Costing defaults), so the tab shows it only as the breakdown's VAT row.
  - **Each metric has a percentage box and an amount box**: a `PercentTextBox` (`PercentDecimalDigits="1"`, `PercentPositivePattern="1"` for "25.0%"; the default `PercentEditMode`, `DoubleMode`, takes `PercentValue` in percent, where `PercentMode` read 35 as a fraction) and a `DoubleTextBox` (`NumberDecimalDigits="2"`) bound to `EditingLaborAmount`, `EditingOverheadAmount`, `EditingAdministrativeAmount` or `EditingMarkupAmount`. **Those settings go on each box, never in a `Style`**, which would replace the theme's.
  - **The percentage is the only thing kept**: an amount's getter is the cost it comes to (`Costs.Labor` and so on, raised again on every `Costs` change), and its setter sets the percentage that comes to it, rounded to the costing columns' 4 decimals (`TypedAmount.PercentFor`). Worked out again from that percentage, the amount can come back a hair off what was typed (23.77 as 23.7699…), which the binding would push into the box under the cursor, so `TypedAmount.Shown` hands back the typed amount while the percentage and its cost are the ones it was typed against. With no cost to take a percentage of, the percentage stays and the box goes back to 0.00. Tests: `TypedAmountTests`, `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests.Each_metric_s_value_follows_its_percentage_and_a_value_typed_sets_the_percentage_Save_keeps`.
- **The breakdown**, each row with its amount and % of the price: the sub-assemblies, then each section in recipe order, indented, smaller and muted under their total, named as its tab is (`ProductCostSummary.SubAssemblyLines`); Wastage, labelled with Settings' percentage ("Wastage (3% of sub-assemblies)", read-only); packaging; labor, overhead and administrative (each labelled with its percentage); the subtotal, the base cost, highlighted (`AccentSoftBrush`), where the breakdown ends. A composite's reads its Products (ingredients), with each product under it as "Ensaymada × 6", and "% of products".
- **The price comparison**, a second table under the breakdown, so the suggested price can be read against the store's: Component, then the Suggested Selling Price, its heading named for the unit (`ProductsViewModel.SuggestedSellingPriceLabel`: "Suggested Selling Price per Piece", "per Unit of 8 Pieces", a composite's "per Unit"), then the Current Store Price (the header's Current Price). Its rows are Markup % (the Markup Metric beside `CurrentMarkupPercent`), Markup (`Markup` beside `CurrentMarkup`), VAT (`Vat` beside `CurrentVat`) and the selling price, highlighted (`SellingPrice` beside `CurrentPrice`). The store price's VAT is taken out first (`CostingRules.PreVatPriceFrom`, `ProductCostSummary.CurrentPreVatPrice`), as the header's Markup % does. Its Markup % and Markup are amber below the Markup Metric and red below the base cost (`IsBelowTargetMarkup`, `IsCurrentPriceBelowCost`), as the header's are; with no current price its cells are dashes. When Settings sets a price rounding, a last row carries the rounded price in the suggested column (`PriceRoundingLabel`, `RoundedSellingPrice`; Domain Model, Costing defaults). The shares of the price stay in the breakdown and under the metrics: the comparison has no % of Price column.
- **A new product takes Settings' costing defaults** (`ApplyDefaults`, `ShowsDefaults`, `SetDefaults`), and follows a change to them while it's untouched, through a rebase rather than an edit (Undo, above). `ProductCosting` also holds the costing as loaded (`IsChanged`, what Save compares).

## One Save writes the whole product

- **Any open form is applied first**, then `ProductValidator` and `CostingParametersValidator` run.
- **The form is compared with what was loaded** (the product's recipe, its packaging and its costing), and `IProductRepository.SaveAsync(product, recipe?, packagingLink?, packagingLines?, costing?)` gets only what changed: the whole `RecipeDraft` (its own sections with their lines, and its links, each with its piece size) or null; the packaging link (`PackagingLinkDraft`, Domain Model) or null; the packaging lines or null; the costing or null.
- **One unit of work** (`VersionedSaves.SaveProductAsync`): the product (added or updated from a copy of its fields, stamping `UpdatedAt`), its recipe, packaging and costing, then its version and the versions of the products sharing what it changed, then the commit (Domain Model, Product versions). Each step goes through the `internal static` write methods the single-purpose repository methods share (`ProductRepository.AddAsync`/`UpdateAsync`, `ProductSubAssemblyUsageRepository.SetRecipeAsync`, `PackagingLineRepository.SetLinesAsync`, `CostingParametersRepository.SetAsync`), so every check runs and a refusal anywhere rolls everything back, sections included. The caller's entities are never attached, so a rolled-back save leaves no id on them. `ProductCloner` adds its product through the same `AddAsync`.
- **Afterwards `LoadCommand` runs and a fresh copy of the product is selected** (`ShowSavedProduct`, with the product and detail the Save read back inside its unit of work), so the form and its baseline are what was saved. `LoadCommand`'s reads go at once on the thread pool.
- **Save finishes its reload before it returns.** Until the reload shows them linked, the form still holds the sections it moved to the Sub-Assemblies screen as new, so a second Save in that moment would keep each twice. The saved detail is handed to the selection's load (`_preloadedDetail`), which shows it without awaiting, so the form closes and opens again in one pass, with no flicker and nothing left to arrive.
- **Linking, copying and Make a Copy of a section, and the packaging link, are edits, saved by Save.** Clone and Create Variant, the list's moves and renames, and Delete act at once.
- Tests: `ProductSavePersistenceTests`, and a `ProductsViewModelRefreshTests` test that fills in a new product and saves it with one Save.

## Unbuffered costs

- **The cost basis button on the header** (`ToggleCostBasisCommand`, between Print and Delete) switches every cost on the screen between the materials' `BufferedCostPerBaseUnit` and their true `CostPerBaseUnit`. **It's named for the costs it switches to**: Unbuffered on the buffered costs, Buffered on the true ones (`CostBasisButtonLabel`, `CostBasisButtonTooltip`), and while the true costs show an **Unbuffered costs** badge sits after the title. It isn't a checkable button: a pressed button reading Buffered contradicts itself, and a checkable `ButtonAdv` flips `IsChecked` itself on a mouse press with `SetValue`, which drops a one-way binding.
- **For the session, not saved.** `ProductsViewModel.CostBasis` (`Domain/Enums/CostBasis`: `Buffered`, `Unbuffered`) isn't part of the product, its snapshots or `History`, so the switch adds no undo step and doesn't offer Save. It stays from one product to the next and through Revert; every launch starts buffered.
- **Print follows it**, This product and a set alike (`PrintTabViewModel.PrepareSetAsync` reads at the screen's basis), and the page says so: " · Unbuffered costs" after the title's cost caption and in the Costing heading (`ProductPrintout.CostBasis`, `ProductPrintLayout.BasisSuffix`). **Reports stay buffered.**
- **How it reaches every figure**: `RawMaterialRules.CostPerBaseUnitAt(material, basis)` is the one place that picks the cost.
  - The immutable section types hold both: `SectionLine.CostAt`/`CostPerGramAt` and `ProductUsageEdit.TotalCostAt`/`CostPerPieceAt`, worked out in its constructor. So a switch rebuilds nothing, the undo snapshots and saved-versus-edited comparisons are untouched, and `RecomputeCosts` reads the basis.
  - The live rows (`IngredientLineRow`, `PackagingLineRow`) have a settable `CostBasis`, `Buffered` by default, so Raw Materials and Sub-Assemblies are unchanged. A `PackagingLineRow` prices its unit choices again and keeps its unit. The screen sets the basis on every row put in the form's tables and the Packaging tab (`OnItemAdded`: a read, an undo, a form opened), in the add rows' factories, and on every row when it's switched.
  - A composite's products: `SavedProductReader` prices each product both ways from the one read (`ComponentCosts`: recipe and packaging, buffered and unbuffered; `ReadComponentCostsAsync`), and `ProductComponents` resolves its rows at the basis, so a switch reads nothing again.
  - The pickers' pop-ups (the form's ingredient, add-in and packaging pickers, and the Packaging tab's) each have a Buffered and an Unbuffered cost column, one hidden: `controls:CostBasisColumns.Basis`, bound on the `SfDataGrid`, hides the one that doesn't match (`CostBasisColumns.Shows` marks them), since a `GridMultiColumnDropDownList`'s columns aren't in the visual tree and can't bind.

## Composite products

The domain side, what a composite is and how it's priced, is in the Domain Model's Composite products.

- **Making one**: New's arrow offers New composite product (`NewCompositeCommand`), its items flattened `ButtonAdv`s that close the drop-down on click, as Add Sub-Assembly's do; the list's folders offer New Composite Product Here, and its empty space New Composite Product (`NewCompositeProductInCommand`). `IsEditingComposite` is set with a product's details (`SetComposite`), and a new one's by `StartNewProduct(isComposite)`. The header reads "New composite product", and the costs "Per unit". **Quantity Required hides** (its column takes no width, `BooleanToStarWidthConverter` with "0.5", and the gap after it is its box's margin, so the gap goes too).
- **The Products tab** (`ComponentsRecipeTab`, the Composite icon) comes first, before the composite's own sections' tabs: `MirrorUsageTabs` mirrors `UsageEdits` index for index after it (`SectionTabOffset`), and the add tab goes after the sections (`InsertAddSubAssemblyTab`). A composite opens on its Products tab, and deleting its last section falls back to it.
  - **`ComponentsSection` over `ProductComponents`** (the screen's `ComponentsTable`, since an undo snapshot's initializer already names `Components`) is typed straight into, like the Packaging tab: Product (a `GridMultiColumnDropDownList` over `ComponentProductOptions`), Qty, Recipe Cost and Cost, with Remove Line, and rows drag into order. Qty is an `int?` in a 0-decimal `NumericColumn`.
  - **The rows are `ProductComponentRow`s.** What each product brings is read off the UI thread, once per product, the first time it's picked (`ReadMissingCosts`, so the add row prices itself before it's committed), and again on every `LoadCommand` (`TakeFresh`), so a product repriced elsewhere reaches its lines; `Reprice` prices the lines again when a cost arrives or the basis changes. A reprice isn't an edit: the undo snapshot holds only ids and quantities (`ComponentLineState`, `RecipeTabChoice.Components`). The caption's figure is what the products bring alone (`ComponentsCost`, the Cost column's sum).
  - **The screen watches the rows' `ComponentProductId`, `Quantity`, `RecipeCost` and `PackagingCost` through one `AutoRefreshOnObservable(WhenAnyPropertyChanged(…))`**, not a chained `AutoRefresh` each: each DynamicData `AutoRefresh` takes its own lock, and the subscriber sets the rows' costs, so a cost read landing on a pool thread held one link's lock waiting for another while a load held them the other way round, a deadlock (found in a test run with `dotnet-stack`; the app runs both on the UI thread).
- **Include each product's packaging** is a plain `CheckBox` on Remove Line's row. Its `IsChecked` binds two-way to `IsComponentPackagingTicked`, whose setter makes each change one undo step (`AsUndoStep`) around `EditingIncludesComponentPackaging`, which loading and undo set directly: a step around the box's focus (`FieldUndoStep`) would merge every click made while it kept the cursor, and a `Command` with a one-way `IsChecked` didn't run under UI Automation's Toggle (`ToggleButton.OnToggle`). It's in the form snapshot, so Save offers itself and Revert takes it back.
  - **While it's ticked the table shows a Packaging Cost column**, and each line's Cost is Qty × (Recipe Cost + Packaging Cost). The costing still splits them: the Costing tab's product lines take `ProductComponentRow.RecipeCostTotal`, and the composite's packaging takes each row's `PackagingCostTotal`. The rows carry an `IncludesPackaging` flag, which `SetIncludesPackaging` sets on every row and the add row before the change recomputes the costs. The column is shown and hidden by `Controls/OptionalColumns` (a grid's columns can't bind), which reapplies on the grid's `Loaded` in case the binding lands before the columns exist. Print gives its table the same column (`PrintedComponent.PackagingCost`, `ProductPrintLayout.ComponentColumnsWithPackaging`).
  - The caption above the table (`ComponentsCaption`) and the header's Material Cost tooltip say where the products' packaging goes; the metric tooltips follow the kind (`MetricBaseTooltip`, `MaterialCostTooltip`).
- **Add Sub-Assembly and Delete Sub-Assembly** work as on a product, but the form offers a composite only the product-owned roles and Packaging (`RefreshFormRoleOptions`), and to link or copy only sections in those roles, and packaging (`RefreshLinkableOptions`).
- **Elsewhere**: a composite's row in the list has the bag-of-breads picture; Reports' Pieces is blank for one; and its Delete confirmation says the products it holds stay.
